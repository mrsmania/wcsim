03 More World Cups and collectibles Extend the dataset backwards from 1990: 1986, 1982, and earlier. Content ▸

The dataset covers 1990 to 2022, all nine fully researched. Collectibles grow for free, since collectibility is derived from rating via STICKER_TIERS.

  • Method notes from the 1990-2002 round apply: identical name spellings across tournaments for the same person (personId is the name slug), distinct people must have distinct names, ratings are that-tournament strength on 60-99.
  • Field sizes shrink going back (24 teams in 1982 and 1986, 16 in 1970-1978), so check the group draw and any "32 nations" assumptions first.
  • Period identities keep their own codes, as Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia already do.
  • Remember the server: after any rating change run npm run gen:collectibles then npm run push:collectibles. npm run checks fails while the seed is stale.

Next step: do 1986 end to end as the template for the rest.

22 Quick Run, Career Mode and the three progression systems Which mode feeds the album, the career and the challenges is ad hoc. Decide the rule, then make the app obey one. Structure ▸

The ask (2026-08-19): rework how the two play modes and the three progression systems fit together. Nothing here is broken in the sense of a bug; the problem is that the rules were each reasonable when they landed and were never decided as a set, so the app now answers "what does winning earn me?" differently depending on which door you came through, and there is no stated principle a new feature can be held against. The goal is fewer rules, each one sayable in a sentence.

How it got here. The career design (docs/roguelike-career-design.md, line 206) said the plain game stays as "Quick Play / Exhibition", no boons and no career, with the roguelike layer strictly opt-in. The launcher shipped on that basis (2026-07-09). Since then the album grew a market discount, difficulty grew an album reset, and challenges arrived attached to the Cup Run only. Each was locally sensible. Together they mean Quick Run is no longer an exhibition: it pays into one of the three systems, is charged by a second, and is invisible to the third.

What is actually true today, verified in the code rather than remembered:

  • The mode decides one thing at build time and one thing at kickoff. Both cards lead to the same 3-column build page with both build methods; the route only picks what the single "Start Run" CTA does (App.tsx:647). Quick Run goes to the World Cup (/group, /knockout); Career Mode goes to the Cup Run (/cup-run).
  • The album advances in both modes, the career and the challenges in only one. Banking funnels through a single applyStickers (useStickerAlbum.ts:140) with four triggers, two per mode: the World Cup's loss and cup win (:194, :236) and the Cup Run's (:201, :241). No mode check anywhere in the hook. But applyRunResult is called from exactly one place (CupRunScreen.tsx:284), so XP, Prestige, Ascension and every challenge exist only inside a Cup Run. Winning the World Cup in Quick Run is worth stickers and nothing else, which is not written down anywhere a player can see.
  • Two career perks reach backwards into the build page, both gated on mode: the transfer budget (App.tsx:87, 675) and the extra re-rolls (App.tsx:334). So the build page is mode-aware in two places for two reasons, while the pricing discount below is mode-blind on purpose.
  • The owned-sticker discount applies in both modes (item 02, decided deliberately: the album is global, so a mode-dependent price would have meant the market, the line-up column and the auto-fill spender all needing to know the mode). That decision is still right on its own terms, and it is also the clearest case of Quick Run being charged by a system it cannot advance any faster than Career Mode can.
  • The mode is derived twice. modeOfPath reads it from the route (App.tsx:83), and the reducer separately stores buildMode (gameReducer.ts:60) because the route cannot answer "which page was this build started on" once you have left it. Both are needed today and both are correct, but two sources for one fact is the kind of thing that only stays in step by hand: the challenges kickoff record already has a comment explaining that it must use buildMode and not the route.
  • Changing the difficulty setting wipes the sticker album (App.tsx:156) on the grounds that stickers are scoped to the difficulty they were earned under. Nothing wipes the career or the challenges, which are earned under a difficulty too. Whatever the right answer is, the three should agree.
  • There are two tournament engines. The World Cup (domain/tournament.ts + domain/bracket.ts, driven by TournamentScreen / KnockoutScreen) and the Cup Run (domain/run.ts, driven by CupRunScreen). They share the match sim, the group build, the draw, resolveKoTie and the live clock; they duplicate the round-by-round shell, the result presentation, the outcome model (BracketState.outcome vs RunOutcome + phase), the run-end UI and even the once-per-run sticker guard (GameState.stickersApplied vs RunState.stickersApplied). They also disagree on chemistry: the World Cup scores it by the slot each player fills (App.tsx:523), the Cup Run by each player's natural role (run.ts:191, because a run tracks players and not slots). Same feature, two answers for the same XI. Several of the facts above are downstream of that split rather than of any decision about modes.

Four cross-mode leaks are already reachable, found while surveying this. None is urgent and none is worth a separate item, because each of them dissolves once the questions below are answered rather than being worth patching in place:

  • The budget changes under a build in progress. The live budget reads the route (App.tsx:676) while the recorded one reads stored buildMode (App.tsx:701), so walking from /career-mode to /quick-run mid-build silently moves the cap from $70 plus perk tier to $110, and the run record then reports the career budget. The re-roll allowance is derived twice the same way (App.tsx:332 from the route, 717 from buildMode).
  • /cup-run accepts a Quick-Run-built XI and applies the career's perks and unlocked boosts to it unconditionally (CupRunScreen.tsx:257), so a $110 three-re-roll build can be run as a full career run.
  • /group and /knockout accept a Career-built XI (neither is mode-gated, App.tsx:855, 875), and starting over from either always returns to /quick-run (App.tsx:564) even when the build was a career one.
  • A stale cup win outranks a fresh one. The two reward-picker producers are priority-ordered rather than mode-scoped (useStickerAlbum.ts:236), so an un-applied World Cup win takes precedence over a Cup Run win.

What has to be decided before any code moves. This is a design question first, and a small refactor second. The questions, roughly in dependency order:

  • What is Quick Run for? Three coherent answers: a true exhibition that earns nothing and is charged nothing (closest to the original intent); a shorter on-ramp that earns a reduced share of everything; or the same game as Career Mode with the roguelike layer switched off. Pick one. Most of the rest follows from it.
  • Is the album global or per-mode? It is global today and that is defensible, but if it stays global then the difficulty reset is the odd one out, and if it goes per-mode then the discount, the swap allowance and the completion target all have to learn about modes.
  • Should challenges judge a World Cup? Most of the 130 entries only reference an XI, matches and scorelines, which a World Cup has. The three families that could not survive outside a Cup Run are Ascension (8), Boosts (12) and Career (12), so 98 of the 130 are in principle judgeable either side of the split. If the answer is yes, the judging seam has to move off applyRunResult, which is a career function.
  • Do the two tournament engines converge? Not required to answer the above, but it is the thing that would stop the duplication growing, and item 04 (in-run economy and node variety) will widen the gap if it lands first.

Next step: answer the four questions above in a short design note before touching code, since the code changes are small once the rules are settled and unbounded while they are not. Worth doing before items 04, 05 and 19, each of which adds to the Career side of the split and would have to be redone. Placement: parked here in Next up because it is architectural and every later Career item rests on it; move it to Later if the content work (item 03) should go first.

19 Daily challenges Three rotating picks from the challenge catalogue, at a reward multiplier. Split out of item 01. Career ▸

Split out of item 01 on 2026-08-18, which now ships an entirely permanent catalogue. This is the rotating layer on top of it: a few challenges singled out for a period, paying more than their base award, so there is a reason to build a different XI today than yesterday and so challenges you skipped get re-offered.

Blocked on item 01, not by choice but by construction: this rotates through that catalogue and reuses its predicates, its awards and its CareerState field. There is nothing to build here until the honours exist and are worth completing.

What it adds once they do: a period key, the chosen ids and a reward multiplier on CareerState, picked deterministically from the period so every device agrees without a server round trip. Plus the two surfaces item 01 deliberately left out, which only work over a handful of targeted challenges: the build-page strip ("Today: Rag Tag, United Nations, The Wall") so you can build toward them while picking, and the chips under the run ladder, with a challenge marked already failed the moment it becomes impossible (conceding kills The Wall right there) rather than left looking winnable. The earlier comp of both is in the git history of docs/redesign-2026/turf-flat/challenges.html.

The questions to settle: daily or weekly (daily is a much faster treadmill and pairs naturally with the seeded run in item 05, where everyone gets the same rolls and the same objective; weekly asks less of a player who plays twice a week); whether a missed day is simply gone; whether the pick is filtered to what is actually reachable for that career, since offering "Win at Ascension III" to a level-2 account is noise; and whether a re-roll exists at all, which if it does should cost Prestige rather than be free.

Next step: nothing until item 01 ships. Then decide the period and whether the pool is filtered by reachability, both of which change how it feels far more than the multiplier does.

18 Player versus player, for signed-in accounts Build a team from your career progress and play someone else's. Details to be defined. Accounts ▸

Requested 2026-08-18, shape deliberately open. A signed-in player assembles a team from what their career has actually earned, and plays it against another player's team. The sim already decides matches from two XIs, so the match itself is the part that exists; everything around it is the design work.

What is already in place, and worth knowing before designing it: accounts and the per-account store (album, career, settings, in-progress run) are live, the match and shootout sim take two sides and are deterministic apart from their own randomness, simulateTitleOdds can rate a matchup, and the sticker economy is validated server-side because the browser talks to Postgres directly - the same reasoning will apply to anything competitive.

The questions to settle first, roughly in the order they bite:

  • What is a "team based on career progress"? The collected album only? Career budget plus perks? A separate roster you maintain? This decides whether PvP is a showcase of progress or a second economy.
  • Live or asynchronous? Asynchronous (submit a team, it plays overnight or on challenge) fits a self-hosted server and a small friend group far better than live matchmaking, and needs no realtime service - which was deliberately trimmed from the NAS stack.
  • Who validates the team? A client-submitted XI is a claim; the server has to check it against what that account actually owns, or the first thing anyone does is submit eleven 99s. This is the same problem finish_run solved for stickers and probably wants the same shape (a security definer function that reads the account's own rows).
  • Fairness. A three-year-old career against a new one is not a game. Tiers, handicaps, or a budget-equalised format all work; pick one before building.
  • What is at stake? Prestige, a ladder, a record, or nothing but bragging. Anything transferable needs anti-cheat thinking; nothing transferable is much cheaper to ship.
  • Guest story. The project rule is guest-first (NFR-1): the whole game is playable with no account. PvP is a legitimate account-only extra, which the requirements already allow for ("may gate genuinely online extras"), but the entry point must degrade cleanly when signed out.

Next step: settle the first two questions (what the team is made of, and asynchronous versus live), since every other decision hangs off them. Then a requirements doc in the shape of docs/cloud-sync-requirements.md before any code.

21 Dead columns on run_results Four columns nothing writes and nothing reads. Decide in a clean-up pass: drop them, or revive the table as a real run archive. Accounts ▸

Found 2026-08-19 while trying to answer "how many runs has this account finished at Ascension II or higher?" from the database. The answer is that it cannot be, and the column that looks like it should say so reads 0 for every row.

run_results was created by 0001 as a genuine per-run archive and populated in full by 0003. 0006_client_surface.sql then narrowed the function deliberately, and for a good reason it states in its own header: banking stickers and computing the career award turned out to be two different moments in two different places, so finish_run kept everything that protects the collection and gave the career award back to the client. The career facts stopped being passed, and the columns holding them went quiet. 0009 and 0010 carried the narrowed insert forward.

Confirmed against the live server, not just the migration files: neither finish_run nor finish_run_v2 takes an ascension, score, rounds-won or XI argument, so the client cannot send them even in principle. The insert is (user_id, run_key, outcome, won_cup, xi) with xi a literal '[]'::jsonb; the rest take their defaults (0, 0, 0). Across 33 rows from two accounts, ascension > 0 matches nothing.

The table still earns its keep and must not be dropped: the unique (user_id, run_key) is what rejects a double submit before a single sticker moves, and import_guest_progress reads it to decide whether an account is empty. Only the four columns are dead weight.

Two ways to go, decided 2026-08-19 to be neither for now, since nothing is broken and the career counters cover what the challenges need:

  • Drop them. Honest, and stops the next person building on a column that is always 0. It is what item 06 nearly did.
  • Revive them. Repopulate on finish and the table becomes the real per-run history: "runs finished at Ascension II" answerable retroactively, and a foundation for the trophy cabinet (item 06) or any leaderboard. Costs a new function name (finish_run_v3): the signature would change, and a changed signature has the same PGRST202 problem a changed return shape had, since migrations are applied by hand while the client ships on push to main. Account-only either way - guests write nothing to the server.

Either way it only ever covers runs from the change onward. The rows already there cannot be back-filled; the facts were never sent.

Opening the catalogue at level 3 sounds like good onboarding, but level 3 arrives around run 8 while the first cup is won around run 4, so 14 to 16 careers in 16 win the cup before the catalogue opens and are paid nothing for the best moment in the game. It changes the economy not at all (24 to 26% at every gate from level 1 to 5) and permanently costs 2 to 4 suppressed challenges. If the onboarding worry returns, the version that works is to keep judging from run 1 and gate only the reveal, paying the backlog as one catch-up at the unlock (about 240 Prestige over 34 challenges at level 3).

  • 10 follow-up: a move can rotate three or more. Done 2026-08-18, after the user asked why an interchangeable front three could not be switched round. Pairwise trades already worked and had shipped, so the report looked wrong at first - but the narrow case behind it is real: where NO pair can trade, a rotation still can. From the dataset, Knoflicek [LW,ST] at LW, Burruchaga [AM,RW,ST] at ST, Donadoni [LW,RW,AM] at RW - each pairwise swap correctly refused (nobody can play the other's slot), yet rotating all three one place round is perfectly legal, so the front three read as stuck. Swept over every formation and 40 random XIs each: 8,801 legal moves, 821 of them (9.3%) reachable only by a rotation.
    canMove became planMove, which returns the whole resulting placement and finds it with the standard bipartite augmenting path (Kuhn): put the mover in his target, then re-place whoever he displaced, recursing through whoever THAT displaces. Each slot is considered once per search, which keeps it linear (~15 us even with eleven players who can all play everything) and finds the smallest rearrangement rather than reshuffling the XI. The empty-slot move and the straight trade are the same rule with chains of one and two, so nothing that worked before changed. MOVE_PLAYER now just stores what planMove returns, so the reducer never has to know the shape of the chain. The badge stops promising a straight trade when it is not one: a rotate glyph and "take X's spot, rotating N players". Verified over 8,801 plans that every result is a valid line-up with nobody dropped or duplicated, plus the rotation and a plain trade driven in a browser.

Both halves checked in a browser: no inert pulse survives with a player held, and an empty destination still lights up and accepts the move.

Taking a card out of the squad list or the market already dropped a move in progress, but the reverse did nothing at all - with a card in hand, tapping a placed player was inert, so the only way out was to put the card back first. The cancel effect had to stop watching the selection at the same time, or a move would have cancelled itself the instant it cleared the card. Checked in a browser both ways, and that a held collectible still shows "swap in for" on the two slots it fits while the other badges offer a move.
  • 10 A placed player can move within his position range. Done 2026-08-18. Tap his badge with empty hands and the slots he can take light up: empty ones as a "+", team-mates he can trade places with as an amber swap badge. A trade needs BOTH halves to fit: the occupant has to be able to play the slot being vacated, or the pair is refused rather than quietly producing an invalid line-up. A held card (a drawn squad card or a market player) suppresses the whole thing, so placement and collectible-swap targets keep the pitch to themselves.
