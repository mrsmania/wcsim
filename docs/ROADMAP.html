03 More World Cups and collectibles Extend the dataset backwards: 1986 shipped 2026-08-23 (24 nations, 529 rows, 3 new collectibles). Next: 1982, then earlier. Content ▸

The dataset covers 1990 to 2022, all nine fully researched. Collectibles grow for free, since collectibility is derived from rating via STICKER_TIERS.

  • Method notes from the 1990-2002 round apply: identical name spellings across tournaments for the same person (personId is the name slug), distinct people must have distinct names, ratings are that-tournament strength on 60-99.
  • Field sizes shrink going back (24 teams in 1982 and 1986, 16 in 1970-1978), so check the group draw and any "32 nations" assumptions first.
  • Period identities keep their own codes, as Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia already do.
  • Remember the server: after any rating change run npm run gen:collectibles then npm run push:collectibles. npm run checks fails while the seed is stale.

1986 shipped 2026-08-23, and the template it set is mostly about the SOURCE. The nine existing tournaments were verified against Wikipedia's squad lists; this environment's egress proxy blocks Wikipedia, worldfootball.net and footballdatabase.eu alike, and WebSearch returns snippets rather than rosters. What does work is git clone through the session's git proxy, so the rosters came from openfootball/world-cup (1986--mexico/squads.txt, CC0, so nothing to attribute). It carries 1982, 1978 and earlier too.
It was validated rather than trusted: against our own independently-researched 1990, 507 of 528 names match and every one of those carries the identical shirt number. The 21 misses are spelling variants, not disagreements. Two sources agreeing on 507 shirt numbers is the provenance check.
What the source does not carry has to be authored: it has only GK/DF/MF/FW, so the twelve specific positions are assigned by hand (accurately for known players, and as a distribution that guarantees every squad has full-backs, a holder and wide players for the rest), and the ratings are a judgement call as always - baseline-plus-spread for the players with no surviving record, because 22 identical ratings would make a whole side one interchangeable block.
The costs beyond the rows: three new nations needed flags and confederations (HUN, IRQ, NIR - all three ship in country-flag-icons, Northern Ireland as the home-nation GB-NIR), and the front page's "since 1990" copy moved to 1986. A 24-team field needed nothing: 1990 and 1994 already prove it, so the warning above about "32 nations" assumptions did not bite.
Six personId collisions came in with it and one was already shipped: Brazil's 1986 Oscar, Júnior and Júlio César, Spain's Víctor, Mexico's Javier Hernández (Chicharito's father) and Portugal's João Pinto all share a display name with a different, later person, and each took an explicit override. Hunting them found "Carlos Aguilera" already merging Uruguay's 1990 striker with a different Spaniard from 1998 - a live bug, fixed in the same commit. Expect this every tournament, and check it before believing a dataset addition is done: validateSquads does not catch it, because two people with one name is indistinguishable from one person with two appearances.
Two things 1986 left open are item 33: the regenerated collectible seed is committed but not yet pushed to the account server, and 255 of the 529 rows carry an inferred position and a baseline rating rather than an authored one.
New collectibles: 3, taking the album from 81 to 84 - Maradona 1986 at 98, which is a Monumental and the second-highest card in the game behind Messi 2022, plus Platini and Lineker at 90. All three wear number 10.

18 Player versus player, for signed-in accounts Build a team from your career progress and play someone else's. Details to be defined. Accounts ▸

Requested 2026-08-18, shape deliberately open. A signed-in player assembles a team from what their career has actually earned, and plays it against another player's team. The sim already decides matches from two XIs, so the match itself is the part that exists; everything around it is the design work.

What is already in place, and worth knowing before designing it: accounts and the per-account store (album, career, settings, in-progress run) are live, the match and shootout sim take two sides and are deterministic apart from their own randomness, simulateTitleOdds can rate a matchup, and the sticker economy is validated server-side because the browser talks to Postgres directly - the same reasoning will apply to anything competitive.

The questions to settle first, roughly in the order they bite:

  • What is a "team based on career progress"? The collected album only? Career budget plus perks? A separate roster you maintain? This decides whether PvP is a showcase of progress or a second economy.
  • Live or asynchronous? Asynchronous (submit a team, it plays overnight or on challenge) fits a self-hosted server and a small friend group far better than live matchmaking, and needs no realtime service - which was deliberately trimmed from the NAS stack.
  • Who validates the team? A client-submitted XI is a claim; the server has to check it against what that account actually owns, or the first thing anyone does is submit eleven 99s. This is the same problem finish_run solved for stickers and probably wants the same shape (a security definer function that reads the account's own rows).
  • Fairness. A three-year-old career against a new one is not a game. Tiers, handicaps, or a budget-equalised format all work; pick one before building.
  • What is at stake? Prestige, a ladder, a record, or nothing but bragging. Anything transferable needs anti-cheat thinking; nothing transferable is much cheaper to ship.
  • Guest story. The project rule is guest-first (NFR-1): the whole game is playable with no account. PvP is a legitimate account-only extra, which the requirements already allow for ("may gate genuinely online extras"), but the entry point must degrade cleanly when signed out.

Next step: settle the first two questions (what the team is made of, and asynchronous versus live), since every other decision hangs off them. Then a requirements doc in the shape of docs/cloud-sync-requirements.md before any code.

30 A boost catalogue that is not "strength + X" CLOSED 2026-08-23. 32 cards ship; 164 ideas were rejected across seven rounds, and five shipped cards were deleted - three for being worse than a free starter, then Full-Backs and Ice Veins. docs/boost-ideas.md. Career ▸

The ask (2026-08-22): more boosts, and creative ones - cards that are not only "+N to some subset of the XI". docs/boost-ideas.md holds them, grouped by which lever each pulls, with a build cost against each.

Round 1: 3 of 25 built. Away Days (-5 to the next opponent's defence) and Man-Marking (-5 to their attack) are a deliberate pair, and the first cards to touch the OPPONENT at all. Double Print (a cup win picks two stickers) is the first to reach the album.

Round 2: 4 of 25 built, each on a different surface. Prime Years turns every player into his own best tournament, walking personId - which links the same human across cups and which nothing else had ever read. In Form (+12 to the leading scorer this run) reads the run's own tally. Old Guard brings the career's all-time top scorer into the XI. And The Armband is the first card in the game that asks a question: name your captain, +6 to him and +1 to the rest.

Two things came out of round 2 that outlive its cards. A real exploit was closed: grantBoon recorded only the FIRST incoming player in boostedIds, which is all a one-for-one swap has - but Prime Years swaps up to eleven, so ten upgraded players would have banked as stickers they were handed rather than drafted, which is the exact hole that field exists to close. And the balance harness now hands every card a FIRING context: without one, a conditional card measures 0.0 and goes unbanded, which is precisely how an over-band card gets into the pool. It caught two immediately - The Armband at 3.3 against a rare's 3.2, and Old Guard at 4.8.

The Armband also left a seam behind. Boon.choice parks RunState.pendingChoice instead of committing the stop, and resolveChoice applies the card once the player answers - so a reload lands back on the question rather than losing the card. A whole family of cards is cheap now that was not expressible yesterday: ones whose effect the player AIMS. The first five ideas of round 3 are all of that shape.

Round 4: 3 of 25 built, and each opened something. Full-Backs (+8 to your left-back and right-back) was the first card that read the twelve POSITIONS rather than the four categories. It shipped on the claim that every formation plays both, so it always finds its two players; that claim was false and the card was deleted 2026-08-23 - see the closing note below. Underdog's Purse (+2 to the XI for every round you went in as the lower-rated side) is the first that reads the run's own HISTORY - worth nothing at the first stop, and worth nothing at all to a run that was favourite every time.

And Loan Deal is the first temporary ROSTER change in the game. The effect ledger has always handled temporary ratings and has never handled temporary people, so the run records the loan (RunState.loan) and prepareKnockoutRound hands the borrowed player back when the round advances. Two things fell out of it. It is a common starter and Poach is the rare, because the balance table measured the two at 4.0 and 3.9: at the same rarity this would have been Poach with an expiry date, which is a strictly dominated card. And commitBoon had to be restructured - a RunModifier used to run over the run as it was and then have roster/effects written over the top, so the first modifier that needed to see its own card's swap would have had its change silently overwritten. A neighbouring bug went with it: Scout Network dropped a starter boost's run modifiers entirely, so a free Ice Veins at kickoff did nothing at all.

Round 4 went at the two things every earlier round missed: moving rating between players rather than adding it (Squad Harmony flattens the XI to its own average; Meritocracy widens the gap; Wage Bill moves 10 from the best to the two worst), and multiplying rather than adding (a percentage helps a strong XI most, where a flat number helps a weak one most - a genuinely different distribution, and nothing in the game scales). Both change the SHAPE of an XI at a roughly fixed total, which is a different question from anything in the pool.

Diminishing returns are visible and worth saying. Four rounds in, the reject list (71) is more than twice the catalogue (30), and the levers this engine exposes are a short list that has now mostly been proposed at least once. A round 5 would be better aimed at a NAMED surface - "cards about the goalkeeper", "cards for a run that is losing" - than at another open-ended 25.

Round 3 was 25 ideas, and the discard list is now the most useful part of the document - 46 ideas have been considered and rejected across three rounds, and they are all named so they do not come back. Beyond the question cards, round 3 goes at results that have not happened yet (a card contingent on winning this tie), the offer itself (a card about the pool), formation and shape (never touched), the dataset's extremes (shirt numbers, oldest and newest tournaments), and undoing - every card in the pool so far only adds, and nothing removes.

Round 5 took the advice above and aimed at four named surfaces rather than another open-ended 25: the goalkeeper (one of eleven, and only Keeper Coach has ever addressed him), a run that is LOSING (every card in the pool is priced for a run that is winning), the shape Loan Deal opened (temporary people, now that the machinery exists), and the stop itself (the offer, the re-roll, the moment of choosing).

Round 5: 1 of 25 built. Siege Mentality (+1 to the XI for every goal conceded this run) is the first card that pays a run going badly, and the first that reads a scoreline rather than a rating. Goals come off run.history in both halves - a knockout record's oppGoals, a group record's three matchday scorelines - and a shootout is excluded by construction, since kicks live in KoMatch.pens and never in a scoreline. It is +1 a goal rather than +2 deliberately: at +2 a deliberately poor defence would have been worth farming.

Round 6 kept the named-surface method and took four the catalogue had never touched at all: the rest of the tree (fourteen other teams play every round and no card had ever looked at them), who scores (POSITION_WEIGHT decides the name beside a goal, and the cabinet now has a top-scorer board reading it), chemistry (six categories and a capped bonus that nothing in the pool moves), and the payout (Mortgage the Future was the only card whose cost landed on the career).

Round 6: 3 of 25 built, all three from the payout. Sponsorship doubles the XP and leaves the wallet alone, which makes it the only card that touches the one thing Prestige cannot buy: the dearest perk tiers are level-gated and challenge awards grant no XP, so playing is the only route through. Youth Development is its mirror - it empties the wallet and banks a starter boost for the NEXT run. And All or Nothing is Mortgage the Future with the failure narrowed to a single round: triple on a cup, nothing at all on a lost final, and every other exit paying exactly what it would have.

The four payout cards are now resolved in one place in applyRunResult, because they compose: a "pays nothing" test, a multiplier, and then the two opposite halves (one multiplies the XP, one empties the wallet). Youth Development's grant rides on CareerStats.bonusStartBoosts rather than on CareerState - the same trick the challenge counters and the run archive used, since save_career merges stats as jsonb and silently drops top-level keys it does not know. So it needed no SQL. It is spent in startAndPlayGroup, cleared in the same career write that remembers the Ascension tier, and dealt from commons only alongside Scout Network's.

Round 7 keeps the method and takes four more untouched surfaces: the group stage (three matches, a table and a finishing position that no card reads), the match itself (the scoreline, extra time, the shootout's own rules), the dataset as history (nine tournaments, real nations, real years, and only Prime Years has ever walked it), and the XI as a squad rather than as two averages.

The problem in plain English. You run npm run checks and it reports one failure. You change nothing and run it again, and it passes. The suite is not testing anything differently the second time; it just played a different random run. This is worse than having no check at all, because it teaches you to re-run until the output is green, and once that is the habit a REAL failure reads as the usual flake and gets waved through. The repo already knows this - the boon-power block seeds its randomness for exactly this reason, and the note beside it says a randomly-failing assertion is worse than none - so this is a rule the codebase set and then broke somewhere else.

Which check, and what it does. career: run rewards accrue and account correctly (near the end of scripts/checks.ts). It plays one whole Cup Run for real, with unseeded Math.random, always taking the FIRST boost of each offer, and then asserts that the career banked the run correctly: runs went up by one, the level matches the XP, a cup shows up as a cup, xpGained equals run.score, and prestigeGained is at least 1.

Why it fails. Two of those assertions were written when every boost only changed ratings, and the payout cards that shipped on 2026-08-23 broke both. Since the suite runs the block once, that is the odds of a red run.

The same trap is armed a second time, and today it happens to be unreachable. None of them fired in 2000 runs - not because the assertion is safe, but because all three are bought with Prestige and INITIAL_CAREER has none, so they are not in this check's pool. Fix only the XP line and the Prestige line stays a landmine for whoever makes one of them a starter.

Measured 0 failures in 3000 runs, against 541 of 2000 before, and ten consecutive green suites.
Seeding the block was the alternative and was rejected: it makes the failure deterministic but freezes ONE path through the payout rules, which is the opposite of what a characterization suite is for. 32 Two boost decisions a thorough test turned up CLOSED 2026-08-23, both halves the same day. Offers now exclude what the run already holds, and Full-Backs - dead in 6 of the 24 formations - was deleted rather than retargeted. Career ▸

Where this came from. The question was simply "do all the boosts fire, and are they drawn at random across the knockout phase once purchased?". The answer to both is yes - all 34 cards change something when applied, every stop is a fresh rarity-weighted draw, every unlocked card is reachable, no card appears twice inside one offer, the offer widens with Extra Choice and Scout Network deals its free commons. All of that is asserted now. These two are what the testing found on the way, and both are balance decisions rather than defects.

1. Full-Backs is dead in a quarter of the formations. The card gives +8 to the left-back and the right-back, and its own comment claimed every formation plays both, "so it always finds its two players and never needs a no-op branch". That is false. All three styles of 3-4-3 and 3-5-2 field three centre-backs with wide MIDfielders, so 6 of 24 formations have no full-back at all and the card does literally nothing in them. It is a paid rare (30 Prestige), so for a player who likes a back three it is a dead slot in every offer it ever appears in - exactly what the item-30 deletion rule exists to catch ("a card you spend Prestige to unlock must not be worse than one you already own for free"; a card that does nothing is worse than anything).
Three ways out, and none is obviously right: (a) widen the target to the two widest players in the defensive line, so a back three's outside centre-backs qualify - keeps the card alive everywhere but blurs what it says; (b) retarget to the flanks (LB/RB, else LM/RM as wing-backs) - true to the football, but it moves +8 onto MIDfielders, which lands in the attack average and needs re-banding; (c) leave it and accept that one card rewards a back four, which is a legitimate build-shapes-matter choice as long as it is deliberate rather than an accident of a wrong comment. The comment is corrected either way, and the checks now assert the honest relationship (it fires exactly when the XI plays a full-back) and print how many shapes it is dead in.

2. A card already taken can be taken again. An offer is drawn from "starters plus unlocked" with no exclusion of what this run already holds, so the same card comes round again: measured at 3.7% of offer slots, and a player picking at random doubled up on 4.4% of stops. Nothing documents this either way, which is the reason it is here - the interactions split three ways and two of them are bad:

  • It stacks, sometimes multiplicatively. Sponsorship twice is xpMult 2 then 4, so 4x XP; Ice Veins twice is +16 to the penalty takers; Golden Generation twice is +4/+4. A player who is offered Sponsorship twice should obviously take it twice, which is a lever no card was priced against.
  • Or it is completely wasted. Mortgage the Future, Youth Development, All or Nothing and Double Print are idempotent (booleans, and a Math.max), so a second copy does nothing at all - a dead pick at a stop that only comes round four times in a run.
  • Or it is fine. A second Poach or Wildcard Legend is another player, which is a real choice and arguably the interesting case.
So "exclude what the run already holds" is the obvious fix and it would remove the third case along with the first two. The alternative is to exclude only the cards that cannot benefit (the idempotent ones) and price the stacking deliberately.

DONE 2026-08-23: the offer excludes what the run holds. One helper (offerPool in domain/run.ts) behind all three offer sites, the Physio Table reroll included. A card parked in pendingChoice is not excluded until resolveChoice commits it, since until then it has not been applied and its offer is already gone.
The third case went knowingly: a second Poach or Wildcard Legend was a real choice, and one rule is worth more than three special cases carved around four idempotent cards.

  • Rebuilding the run pool restarted the squad scan at SQUADS[0] every time, so 30 attempts were 30 runs of ONE national side. Prime Years read 1-in-30; the dataset says 78% of best XIs hold a player with a better tournament, and against varied squads it measures 83%.
  • Sample XIs came from bestEleven, which is just the eleven highest-rated players in a squad - no formation, so frequently no keeper and no full-back. That is what made Keeper Coach and Full-Backs look conditional.
  • Even with a formation, the XI has to go through placedPlayers, because placing a player promotes the slot's role onto positions[0] and the position cards read positions[0].
  • A denominator counted runs that never reached a boon stop at all, and a stacking probe applied its first card to a run that had gone out in the group, where chooseBoon is a documented no-op - so every "once" figure was really zero.
The lesson worth keeping: when a card looks conditional, suspect the XI before the card. A real line-up is a formation filled through placedPlayers, from a varied squad, on a run that actually reached a stop.
