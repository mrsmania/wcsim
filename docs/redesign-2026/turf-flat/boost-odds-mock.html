Odds at each decision

The item asks for one thing: run simulateTitleOdds per offered boost rather than once for the current XI, and show the delta on the card, so a pick is informed rather than a guess. This page draws that, and two alternatives, on one real offer.

It is worth drawing rather than just building, because the literal version is measurably wrong on a third of the catalogue. Every figure below was measured against the shipped domain code, at the stop the screens show: an 81-rated budget XI, Base Ascension, through the group, Brazil 2002 drawn in the Round of 16.

What a naive per-card delta prints

All 32 boosts, run through applyBoon and simulateTitleOdds exactly as the item describes, across three budget-built XIs.

  1. 11 of 32 move the cup figure by less than 1.5 points, which is inside the noise of the 600-sim readout the app already ships (±0.9pp).
  2. +21.3pp is what Sold Out Stadium reads, the best number on any card in the pool. Its debt (−6 the round after) is invisible to the measurement path, which skips a startsIn plan.
  3. +0.4pp is what Away Days reads, because a whole-tournament sim replays a random tournament. It does not know who you play next. On the tie it was actually taken for, it is worth +10.7pp.
  4. 850ms of blocked main thread for a baseline plus four cards at 600 sims - and 600 sims is the precision that cannot separate a real 1pp from nothing.

Two of those four are fixed by the same change, and it is not a UI change: the odds have to start from the run as it stands - this bracket, this next opponent, the rounds that are left - instead of restarting the tournament from a group you have already won. That also makes a temporary card temporary, which is what turns Sold Out Stadium from the best card on the board into the honest one. Options 2 and 3 below assume it; option 1 is the item as literally written.

Three cards a number cannot price, and one still counting

The fourth card is the loading state: the tie figure lands in one frame, the cup figure a few hundred milliseconds later. Cards are pickable throughout - the readout never gates the choice. Both bars share one scale (0 to 60%), which is what lets the R16 row and the Cup row sit above each other at all: two rows on two scales would be a chart that lies.

  • The two horizons disagree, and that disagreement is the feature. Sold Out Stadium reads +27 on the tie and level on the cup: the card says so in words, and now the screen says so in numbers.
  • Whole points, never decimals. At 6,000 sims the standard error is around ±0.6pp, so a decimal would be inventing precision. Anything inside ±1pp prints level rather than a fake +0.
  • A bar, not just a figure. The grey run is where you stand, the green is what the card adds. Four cards compare at a glance without reading eight numbers.
  • Cards outside the sim get a label, not a zero. Three shapes cover the catalogue: pays after the run, reaches the album, deliberately hidden.
  • It costs a new sim path and a worker. Roughly 190ms per card at 6,000 sims; five of those on the main thread is most of a second of jank at exactly the moment the screen should feel responsive.

Why it survives the narrow column

The strip is two rows of 7px bar under text that already wraps to three lines, so a card grows by about 34px. The offer is one column below 540px in any case, and the figure it adds is the one thing on the card that does not need reading twice.

The switch stays. "Odds on" in the strip above the cards is a persisted preference, like showFullDraw. Some players want the guess; and turning it off is also the answer for anyone who finds a solved offer less interesting than an unsolved one.

The whole catalogue, position-blind

Eleven of the 32 cards move the cup figure by less than 1.5 points: Keeper Coach, Star Signing, Veteran Core, Away Days, All or Nothing, Youth Development, Sponsorship, Double Print, Old Guard, Kind Draw, Man-Marking. Four of those genuinely pay outside the sim; four are next-tie or next-draw levers the blind method cannot see; three are small cards whose real delta is simply under the noise floor. Only the third group is a presentation problem. The other eight are a measurement problem, and no amount of card design fixes them.

The readout the app already ships has the same fault. CupRunScreen computes simulateTitleOdds(activeXi, 600, ...) with no reference to the run's position, so the "Title" figure in the XI panel still includes a group stage you won two rounds ago, and never mentions who you are about to play. Whatever this item builds, that figure should read from the same place.

Option 2 is the cheap first half of option 3, not a rival to it. The tie figure needs no worker, no loading state and no new sim path worth the name, and it is the half that fixes the opponent cards. Shipping it alone is a real improvement; adding the cup row afterwards changes no markup that option 2 did not already need.

What it would take

  • domain/odds.ts gains a position-aware entry point. Today simulateTitleOdds(players, sims, chem, delta, pool) builds its own group and its own bracket. It needs a sibling that takes the run's bracket and a per-round XI instead: the probe behind this page is 40 lines over buildBracket / playRound / recordRound, all of which are already exported.
  • The per-round XI comes from the effect ledger, not from applyBoon. This is the whole reason the numbers here are right and the naive ones are not: xiOf(roster, effects, round) already knows about appliesFrom and expiresAfter, so asking it for round r gives a card its real lifetime for free. applyBoon is the measurement path and skips startsIn by design; it is the wrong tool here.
  • A boon declares whether it can be priced. A field on the catalogue entry (priced: 'sim' | 'payout' | 'album' | 'hidden') decides between a number and a label. It is one line per card, it lives beside rarity, and it is the thing that stops a payout card reading as worthless.
  • BoostOffer takes an optional odds map keyed by boon id, so the component stays presentational and the offer renders identically with the prop absent - which is what the "Odds on" preference switches, and what a slow first frame shows.
  • A worker, or the cup row is a lie. 190ms per card is fine off-thread and not fine on it. odds.ts's own doc comment already says so ("for a live UI readout prefer a web worker"); this is the caller that finally needs it.
  • The Physio Table re-roll re-runs everything. A fresh offer is three fresh simulations. Worth knowing before wiring the button, and an argument for keeping the tie figure instant.

Three decisions this needs before code

  • Does the readout spoil The Coin Toss? Its result is derived from the XI and the opponent, deliberately, so that reloading cannot re-roll it - which means an odds figure would tell you heads or tails before you pick, and the card is nothing else. Drawn here as "not shown until taken". The alternative is to accept the spoiler and retire the card.
  • Does a solved offer stay interesting? Three cards with numbers on them is an arithmetic problem, not a gamble. The switch is the hedge, but the honest version of this worry is that the strongest card is now always obvious - which is an argument for the tie figure alone, since the two horizons at least disagree with each other.
  • Is Sold Out Stadium still shippable once the screen prices it? Its known hole (free if taken before the final) becomes visible the day this ships. Fix the card, or let the number be the warning.
