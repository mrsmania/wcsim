Concept 4 of five

Dashboard hub

The nav model, in one sentenceHome is a real dashboard with a Continue card and one live tile per system. Nothing is more than two clicks away because everything goes through it.

How it works

Always visible
The crest, which is the way back to the hub, and a Continue pill naming the run in progress. That is the whole chrome.
Where am I
The screen title, plus a back-to-hub crumb. The hub itself is the map, so the chrome does not have to be one.
The hub
A Continue card at the top (the single most likely next action), then tiles for Career, Album, Challenges, Cabinet, Squads and Settings, each showing its own live counter.
Depth
Home, then the destination. Two clicks maximum, one for anything you can see a tile for.
Responsive
The tile grid reflows one to three columns. No nav bar to fit, which is why this is the lightest chrome of the five.

On a phone the case that matters most

The ten findings what this concept does about each

The audit is on the index page. Here is only what this concept changes, including the ones it leaves alone, which are the honest part.

F1

Six navigation mechanisms, none of them complete

One mechanism, the hub, plus the crest as the only global control. The other five mechanisms are deleted rather than replaced.

F2

Challenges is four clicks in, behind a collapsed panel

One click, from a tile that also shows 41 of 130, which is the strongest answer of the five to a catalogue nobody can find.

F3

Two doors lead to the same page

The doors go, and the hub is what replaces them: not a choice between two modes but a Continue card and a Build button.

F4

/cup-run is two screens sharing one route

Career is a tile, the run is what Continue resumes.

F5

Navigation lives inside a content column

The cards were trying to be tiles on the wrong screen. Here they are tiles on the right one.

F6

One column is four different things

Untouched. This concept changes the entry screen and nothing about the build page, which is honest but limited.

F7

There is no persistent "where am I"

Weakest of the five: the crumb and the title only. There is no persistent map, by design.

F8

Reset and back land in inconsistent places

Everything returns to the hub, which is one answer to "go back" instead of three.

F9

Six overlays compete for the same space

Settings becomes a tile and a screen, so five overlays remain.

F10

On a phone the pitch is in the middle

Untouched, same as F6.

What it buys, what it costs

Buys

  • The tiles carry live numbers, so navigation doubles as a progress readout: 34 of 81, 41 of 130, 6 cups. Nothing in the app does that today.
  • The lightest chrome of the five, which leaves the three-column build page and the bracket their full width.
  • The Continue card is the correct primary action almost always, and it is the thing the current launcher buries under two door cards.
  • It is the smallest diff: one new screen, four route gates, and the deletion of the footer nav and the two in-column cards.

Costs

  • Every path goes through home, so moving between two destinations is always two clicks. Album to Challenges is common and would be worse than today.
  • No persistent nav means no persistent orientation, which is finding F7 half unaddressed.
  • A dashboard of tiles is a lobby. It puts a screen between the player and the game every time they open the app.
  • It does nothing for the build page, which is where a player spends the most time and where findings F6 and F10 live.
