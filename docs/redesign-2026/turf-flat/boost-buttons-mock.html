Cup Run · the boost stop

Pressable Boost Cards

The three boosts you choose between are already buttons. Nothing about them says so, and on a phone nothing about them ever will. Five ways to fix that, drawn on one real offer.

What ships today

This is the offer exactly as the game draws it: the first boost of a run, through the group, Brazil 2002 drawn in the Round of 16. Hover it, tap it, and note how little happens.

The finding: every signal it is pressable is a hover signal

At rest a boost card is CARD_FLAT, which is the app's shadowless card: the one the modal sheets use, and the one a panel nested inside another card uses. So the thing you are meant to tap is dressed in the app's own idiom for a passive surface, on a screen where every real card carries the hard shadow.

The two things that do say "control" are a 2px lift and the border turning green, and both fire on hover only. There is no hover on a phone. So on touch, which is where most of a Cup Run gets played, the boost stop offers three passive-looking panels and no affordance whatsoever. Whatever else is chosen below, the fix has to be visible at rest.

Five options

Each is drawn on the same offer, live, so it can be hovered and pressed rather than read about. They are lettered for reference, not ranked; the chips say where each one landed.

A It is raised, and pressing it presses it down Recommended
Pick a boost Next: Brazil 2002 in the Round of 16

What it buys

One token: CARD_FLAT becomes CARD_SM, the 4px hard shadow the trophy cabinet already uses for cards stacked many to a page. It rests raised, so the affordance is there before anybody touches anything. Hover lifts it to the full 6px shadow and pressing drops it flat, moving the card into the space its own shadow was in. That press is the app's own material behaving like a button, and it introduces no new look at all.

What it costs

Three hard shadows in a row is the loudest the boost stop has ever been, and the shadow is pitch-dark green, so the row reads greener than it did. Nothing else: no new element, no new copy, no extra height, and the rarity strip is untouched.

B The card gets a foot Strong second
Pick a boost Next: Brazil 2002 in the Round of 16

What it buys

The card stays a card and the foot is the button, which settles the tension rather than arguing with it: the description goes on describing, and a named action in a chalk strip says what tapping does. The pattern already exists in the app, which is the argument for it. CardDisclosure is a chalk footer set in the mono caption that turns green on hover, so this is a shape the player has met elsewhere.

What it costs

About 28px per card, on a screen a phone already scrolls. And it says the same thing three times: three feet reading "Take this boost" is a lot of chrome for a row whose whole job is to be three choices. Best read as A's understudy rather than as something to run alongside it.

C Pick, then confirm Costs a tap

A boost cannot be given back

What it buys

It ends the argument instead of settling it. The three become a selector, which the house rules already hold apart from the button system ("selectors are not buttons and keep their own shapes"), and the chosen one fills pitch-dark, the one green every chosen cell in the app uses. Then a real btn('primary') does the pressing. It also makes an irreversible pick deliberate, which this screen arguably wants: a mis-tap currently spends the whole round's choice with no way back.

What it costs

A tap, at every one of up to five stops in a run, on the screen's main action. Plus a row of height for the button. It is the only option that changes what choosing is rather than how it looks, so it is a gameplay decision as much as a visual one.

D The rarity becomes a cap, and a corner arrow points out Pairs with A
Pick a boost Next: Brazil 2002 in the Round of 16

What it buys

The 3px rarity line becomes a filled band with the rarity reversed out of it, and a small arrow sits in the corner. It is the cheapest visual change on this page and the only one that fixes a live contrast miss on the way past: the rarity word currently fails AA in both themes, and reversing it out of the fill clears it. See the measurements below. The gold band is the sticker album's own foil, so the top rarity finally looks like the top tier.

What it costs

On its own it is the weakest affordance here: a cap and an arrow are still a flat surface, and the arrow is decoration until something moves. It is a card that looks more deliberate rather than a card that looks pressable, which is why it belongs on top of A rather than instead of it.

E It fills green Not recommended
Pick a boost Next: Brazil 2002 in the Round of 16

What it buys

Unmissable. The whole card becomes the primary button's own fill, with a white label and the description at 6.36 against it. Nobody could look at this row and wonder what to do.

What it costs

It is the primary button, three times, on a screen that also has one. The app has exactly one solid green fill and it means "this is the action here"; spending it on each of three alternatives says all three are. It is also still hover-driven, so it does nothing at all on a phone. Drawn here so the ceiling is visible, not as a candidate.

A second finding, on the same element

While measuring the cards: the rarity word misses AA in both themes as shipped. It is set at 9px bold in the rarity accent, and those three hexes are surface colours borrowed from the sticker tier ramp, deliberately fixed rather than theme-swapped. Computed against the panel they sit on:

Rarity wordHexOn light panelOn dark panelNeeds
Legendary#c99a3a 2.576.494.5
Rare#e4922b 2.496.714.5
Common#15924c 4.004.174.5

The relaxed 3:1 does not apply: that is for text at 18.66px bold or larger, and this is 9px. So two of the three are badly under on paper and the common green is under in both themes.

There is already a precedent for the fix, twice over

--color-amber-ink and --color-pitch-ink exist because amber and green as text are separate values from amber and green as a surface. This is exactly that case: the 3px strip is a surface and keeps the accent, while the word needs the ink. Rare takes amber-ink at 5.92 on panel, common takes pitch-ink at 8.08. Legendary has no gold ink token yet; #7d5f10 measures 5.97 on panel, 5.14 on chalk and 5.33 on ground, so one new token clears all three light surfaces.

Or take option D and it needs no new token, but it does need one deliberate change to the ramp. The existing foil ink #3a2a06 reversed out of the gold band measures 5.40 against the dark end of the foil and 5.58 out of the amber band. The bright common green carries neither that ink (3.47) nor white (4.00), so the common cap has to deepen to #0e5c34, where white reads 8.08. That is the one figure in option D that is not free, and it is the reason the green cap in the specimen above is darker than the strip it replaces.

What any of these has to respect

  • The description stays. "No descriptions inside a button" is a house rule and the boost offer is its one written exception, because here the description is the choice. Any option that shortens a card to a label is out before it is drawn.
  • There is no fourth button design. btn(tone, size) is the whole system and the three tones are a stated ceiling. Every option above either reuses something that already exists (the hard shadow, the chalk foot, the chosen-cell green) or is a selector, which the rules already hold apart from buttons.
  • The class sweep will fail a display-face label in a padded box. npm run checks reads every class-list string in src/ and fails on the app's button voice, font-display plus uppercase, inside a box with its own padding, and on any ! override. So a "TAKE THIS" set in Archivo inside the card is a build failure. The mono caption is uppercase mono and is fine, which is what B's foot uses.
  • The resting state carries the affordance, never the motion. Every transform here is gated on prefers-reduced-motion, so the press and the arrow slide can go away entirely. A raised card at rest and a filled cap at rest both survive that; a hover lift does not, which is the same reason the phone matters.
  • Focus has to be visible. The shipped card sets no focus style at all, so keyboard focus currently lands on it invisibly. Every specimen above has a green outline offset 2px; tab through this page to see it.

What it would touch

src/components/cupRun/BoostOffer.tsx for all five. src/components/cupRun/types.ts if the rarity ink is split from the rarity accent. src/index.css only if a gold ink token is added. scripts/checks/ui.ts if the rarity contrast joins the tone sweep it already runs, which is where it belongs: that sweep computes every button tone from the real tokens and fails under 4.5, and it does not read this element today.
