The premise, corrected: dressing the card up cannot work

The first pass tried a hard shadow, a chalk foot and a filled rarity cap. The shadow one was the worst idea of the three, and for a reason worth writing down: the hard shadow is this game's signature for a card. It is on the standings, the XI sheet, the perk tiles, the trophy shelf, every panel in the app. Putting it on the boost cards makes them look more like all the other cards, not less. It would have made the problem worse while looking like progress.

The general form is the point: a full-width panel with a heading and a line of grey text is what content looks like in this app, and on a phone that is exactly the shape the offer takes. No border, shadow, cap or hover state turns that shape into something a thumb reads as pressable. So the affordance cannot be a treatment applied to the card. It has to be an object that is a button, sitting where the eye already ends up.

And the objection that ruled green out was wrong

The first pass rejected a green fill on the grounds that it is the primary button and the screen already has one. It does not. The picker and the screen's primary button are mutually exclusive: the group screen shows the picker or a Continue button, and in a run the picker is the boost phase while "Play the round" belongs to the phase after it. When the offer is on screen the only other control is the re-roll, which is the quiet tone. So the green is free here, and it is drawn in the stages below with the re-roll underneath so the pair can be judged together.

What it buys

On the phone the answer to "where do I tap" is now the only green thing on the screen, three times, and it measures 46px tall, so it clears the 44px tap target a thumb needs without anything having to grow for it. It is btn('primary') exactly as the rest of the app writes it, so it is not a new design, it is the app's one button turning up where a button was missing. The description keeps its own space above the bar and goes on doing its job.

What it costs

About 45px a card, which on the phone is the whole offer growing by half a screen. Three primary buttons at once is also a first for this app. And it is the loudest of the four: the boost stop becomes the greenest screen in the game, which is arguably right for the most consequential choice in a run.

What it buys

The card and its button are one object rather than a button parked inside a panel, and the card is bracketed by the two things that matter: the rarity across the top, the action across the bottom. It is the most solid of the four on a phone, because the green runs the full width of the card with no paper either side of it. The gold cap is the sticker album's own foil, so the top rarity finally looks like the top tier, and it fixes the contrast miss below on the way past.

What it costs

It is the biggest change to the card's construction here, and the card loses its 6px bottom corners to the bar unless the bar is clipped to them. The common rarity cap also has to deepen to the dark green, which is the one figure in it that is not free (see below), so the ramp's bright green stops appearing on these cards at all.

What it buys

The most conventional mobile shape there is: a list, then one button. It adds the least height of the four (one bar instead of three), the button names what it will take so it can never be pressed blind, and the cards become a selector, which the house rules already hold apart from the button system. It also makes an irreversible pick deliberate: a mis-tap on the current screen spends the whole round's choice with no way back.

What it costs

A tap, at every one of up to five stops in a run. And on a phone the button can be off the bottom of the screen while you are reading the third card, so choosing and confirming happen in two different places. It is the only option that changes what choosing is rather than how it looks.

The contrast miss, which is independent of all four

Found while measuring: the rarity word misses AA in both themes as shipped. It is set at 9px bold in the rarity accent, and those three hexes are surface colours borrowed from the sticker tier ramp. Against the panel they sit on:

Rarity wordHexOn light panelOn dark panelNeeds
Legendary#c99a3a 2.576.494.5
Rare#e4922b 2.496.714.5
Common#15924c 4.004.174.5

The relaxed 3:1 does not apply: that is for text at 18.66px bold or larger, and this is 9px. So two of the three are badly under on paper and the common green is under in both themes.

There is already a precedent for the fix, twice over

--color-amber-ink and --color-pitch-ink exist because amber and green as text are separate values from amber and green as a surface. This is that case exactly: the 3px strip is a surface and keeps the accent, while the word needs the ink. Rare takes amber-ink at 5.92 on panel, common takes pitch-ink at 8.08, and legendary needs one new gold ink value: #7d5f10 measures 5.97 on panel, 5.14 on chalk and 5.33 on ground.

Option C avoids the new token by reversing the word out of a filled cap instead. The existing foil ink #3a2a06 reads 5.40 against the dark end of the gold foil and 5.58 out of the amber band. The bright common green carries neither that ink (3.47) nor white (4.00), so that one cap has to deepen to #0e5c34, where white reads 8.08.

Three things every option here gets right on purpose

  • The bar is not a nested button. The card stays the single control and the bar is a <span> wearing btn(), marked aria-hidden. A button inside a button is invalid HTML and undefined for keyboard and assistive technology, which this app has already been bitten by once: it is why PlayerBadge owns its own gesture rather than being wrapped. The happy side effect is that on a phone the hit area stays the entire card rather than shrinking to the bar, so the button is a signpost and the whole card is the target.
  • It is btn(), so the class sweep is satisfied rather than dodged. The first pass warned that npm run checks fails on the app's button voice inside a padded box. That is aimed at a hand-written button; a bar composed from the shared constant is exactly what the sweep exists to encourage, and it does not read composed strings.
  • Nothing here depends on hover or on motion. Every option is complete in its resting state, which was the whole failure of the first pass. Tab through this page as well: the shipped card has no focus style at all, so keyboard focus currently lands on it invisibly, and every specimen above has a green outline offset 2px.

What it would touch

src/components/cupRun/BoostOffer.tsx for all four. src/components/cupRun/types.ts if the rarity ink is split from the rarity accent. src/index.css only if the gold ink token is added. scripts/checks/ui.ts if the rarity contrast joins the tone sweep it already runs, which computes every button tone from the real tokens and fails under 4.5 but does not read this element today.

What the first pass drew, and why it is not here

Kept as a negative result rather than deleted. The card raised on the hard shadow, which was recommended and was the worst of them: the hard shadow is the app's card signature, so it made the boost cards look more like every other card. A chalk foot with the action in the mono caption, which is a button-shaped hole rather than a button, and on a phone reads as a table row. The rarity strip as a filled cap with a corner arrow, which survives as half of option C but was never an affordance on its own. The card filling green on hover, rejected then for colliding with a primary button that turns out not to be on the screen, and rejected now for the reason that actually matters: it is a hover state, so on a phone it does nothing at all.

Second pass, 2026-09-09, after the first was rejected on the phone. Nothing here is built. The offer is real (Veteran Core, Catenaccio and Galacticos, with their shipped descriptions) and the re-roll below each stage is the real Physio Table control, drawn so the tone question in option B can be judged rather than argued.
