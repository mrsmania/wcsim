The Boost Pick

One screen, two requirements: a control that reads as a button, and numbers that say what each boost is worth. The numbers are what makes a second tap worth taking, which is what merges them.

What is being merged, and why it is one job

Item 65 says the three boosts do not look pressable: on a phone they stack into full-width panels, which is the shape content takes in this app, and every signal that they are controls fires on hover. Item 05 says a pick is currently a guess, and the odds machinery to fix that already exists. Neither is a reason to touch the other, until you notice what each one costs.

Item 65's best remaining option asks the player to tap twice: once to choose, once to confirm. On its own that is friction bought with nothing but safety. And item 05 has the mirror problem: its recommended readout puts two rows of bar on every card, which is height on the screen that can least afford it, and roughly a second of simulation for an offer the player may take in two seconds. Put them together and each pays the other's bill.

The brief, in the owner's words

"Displaying that additional information would justify a 2 click process to select the boost." That is the whole design: the first tap is not ceremony, it is the request for the numbers. Which also settles what happens on the cards themselves, because the numbers no longer all have to be on them.

What is being built on. Button treatments B (a quiet button in every card) and D (choose, then confirm) from the button comp; options A and C are discarded. Readout option 03 from the odds comp: two horizons, position-aware, with a label rather than a zero for the cards a number cannot price.

The offer drawn throughout is the one the odds were measured on: the first boost of a run, through the group, Brazil 2002 drawn in the Round of 16, at four cards because Extra Choice tier 2 makes it four and that is the tallest case. Three is the default.

What it costs

This is the two items shipped next to each other rather than merged, and it pays both bills in full: the phone offer runs to about 640px, so the fourth card is two screens down, and every offer costs the baseline plus four simulations before anything can be shown. It also has no answer for the odds switch being off, where the cards go back to being panels with a button on them.

What it buys

The first tap becomes the question and the panel is the answer, which is the brief. Tap through the four in either frame: the cards stay the size they are today, the phone offer is about 340px instead of 640, and the panel has room for a sentence saying what the two figures mean rather than leaving the player to work out why a card is +27 and level at the same time. It also gives the loading state a natural home: the panel opens and the numbers land in it.

What it costs

Comparing four cards means four taps, and that is the honest cost: option 03's own case was that four cards compare at a glance. The counter is that the taps are free and reversible, nothing commits until the button, and the two horizons have to be read rather than glanced at in any case.

What option 3 does to item 05's bill, which is the real argument for it

Item 05's recommended readout was costed at roughly 950ms for a baseline plus four cards, and it needs a web worker to be honest about it - 190ms a card is fine off the main thread and not fine on it. Computing only the card that was tapped turns that into one 190ms burst per tap, arriving in a panel that has just opened and can hold a loading state without anything looking stuck. That is the difference between needing a worker and probably not needing one. It is a judgement rather than a free win: 190ms is still a visible stall, so it wants measuring on a real phone before the worker is called off.

Two smaller bills go the same way. The Physio Table re-roll becomes free again (a fresh offer computes nothing until something is tapped, where option 03 re-ran three simulations), and the baseline is the only figure that has to be ready before the screen is.

The cards a number cannot price still get a label

Four of the 32 boosts pay outside the simulation, and printing them at +0.0 would say they are worthless when they are not. Option 03's answer carries into all three merges unchanged, and in option 3 it is what the panel says instead of bars:

The three label shapes, on the card and in the panel

A boon declares which of these it is (priced: 'sim' | 'payout' | 'album' | 'hidden', one line beside rarity), and that is the thing that stops a payout card reading as worthless. In option 3 the label is the whole panel for such a card, which is the one place it is cheaper than the other two merges are: no simulation runs at all.

The odds switch, which the merge forces a decision on

Two taps with the odds turned off is friction with nothing behind it

Option 03 keeps an Odds on preference, on the reasoning that some players want the guess and a solved offer is less interesting than an unsolved one. That is right on its own and it collides with this merge: if the numbers are what the second tap buys, turning them off leaves the tap and takes the payment. Three answers, and the third is the recommendation:

Drop the switch - one flow, and the preference the owner might want is gone. Fall back to one tap when the odds are off - honest, and it means the game has two different interaction models for the same screen depending on a setting, which is the kind of thing this codebase deletes rather than adds. Keep both, unchanged, on the ground that the second tap was already justified without the odds: a boost cannot be given back, and today a mis-tap spends the whole round's choice. The odds make the tap pay; they were never the only reason for it.

What the merged item would take

  • A position-aware odds pass. domain/odds.ts gains a sibling to simulateTitleOdds that takes the run's bracket and a per-round XI rather than building its own group. The per-round XI comes from the effect ledger, xiOf(roster, effects, round), which already knows appliesFrom and expiresAfter - that is the whole reason these numbers are right and the naive ones are not. applyBoon is the measurement path and skips startsIn by design.
  • One figure per boost per horizon, requested on demand in option 3, or for the whole offer up front in 1 and 2. Whichever, BoostOffer takes an optional odds map keyed by boon id, so the component stays presentational and renders identically with the prop absent, which is what the switch and a slow first frame both look like.
  • A priced field on the catalogue entry, beside rarity, choosing between a number and a label.
  • The button half of item 65, which is small: the chosen card fills pitch-dark (the app's one chosen-cell green) and one btn('primary') below names what it will take. The bar in merge 1 is a <span> wearing btn() and marked aria-hidden rather than a nested button, for the reason PlayerBadge already carries.
  • The XI panel's own Title figure should read from the same place. CupRunScreen computes simulateTitleOdds(activeXi, 600, ...) with no reference to the run's position, so it still includes a group stage you won two rounds ago. Two figures on one screen disagreeing about the same run is worse than one wrong figure.
  • The rarity word's contrast, carried over from item 65. At 9px bold in the tier accents it reads 2.57 / 2.49 / 4.00 on the light panel and the common green misses in the dark theme too, against the 4.5 a label that size needs. The fix has a precedent twice over: amber-ink and pitch-ink exist because a colour as text is a separate value from the same colour as a surface.

Still undecided, and none of it is settled by this page

  • Does the readout spoil The Coin Toss? Its result is derived from the XI and the opponent so that a reload cannot re-roll it, which means a figure would tell you heads or tails before you pick, and the card is nothing else. Drawn here as "not shown until taken". The alternative is to accept the spoiler and retire the card.
  • Does a solved offer stay interesting? Four cards with numbers on them is an arithmetic problem rather than a gamble. The honest form of the worry is that the strongest card becomes obvious, and the two horizons disagreeing is the only thing working against that.
  • Is Sold Out Stadium still shippable once the screen prices it? Its known hole (free if taken before the final) becomes visible the day this ships. Fix the card, or let the number be the warning.
  • Whether the second tap survives the odds being off - the switch decision above.
