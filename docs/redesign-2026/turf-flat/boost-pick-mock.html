The Boost Pick

One screen, two requirements: a control that reads as a button, and numbers that say what each boost is worth. The numbers are what makes a second tap worth taking, which is what merges them.

How to read it

This section exists because the readout was drawn option 03's way first, and the owner could not read it: "what exactly do the bars say?" and "is +3 = +3% change if this boost is chosen?" Both questions have answers. Neither answer was on the screen, which is a fault in the readout rather than in the reading, so it has changed. Here is the old form, the new one, and the three measurements that decide between them.

What option 03 printed

R16[bar] +11 Cup[bar] +3

The grey run of the bar was your chance as you stand, the coloured run was what the boost adds, and the scale was cropped at 60%, which was stated nowhere. The figure was the added part in percentage points, also stated nowhere, and the thing it was added to was in a strip at the top of the screen rather than on the card.

What it prints now

vs Brazil31% → 41% Cup6% → 9%

Your chance now, and your chance if you take this boost. Two figures, both plainly percentages, both on the card, and the size of the move is the gap between them. Nothing has to be explained and nothing has to be added up.

So to answer the question directly: the old "+3" meant three percentage points, not three per cent better. And that is exactly where it misleads. Away Days' cup row read "+3" against a base of 6.3%, so it took you from 6.3% to 8.8% - a 40% improvement wearing a "+3". Every card's cup row had the same problem, because a percentage-point figure on a 6% base makes a good boost look feeble:

 Base 6.3% to lift the cup. The middle column is the movement as a share of that base, which is the sense in which a boost "improves your chances" and is not what a percentage-point figure says.

Three measurements behind the change

  • A delta and a from-to cannot both be printed at whole precision. This is the one that settles it. Option 03 ruled out decimals, correctly: at 6,000 simulations the standard error is about 0.6pp, so a decimal invents precision. But at whole points the two figures disagree - Away Days is +2 by the measurement and +3 by the printed figures (6% to 9%), Second Wind is +4 and +5, Sold Out Stadium +27 and +28. A screen that prints both is a screen that contradicts itself. So the delta figure goes and the from-to stays, because the from-to is the one that needs no unit explained.
  • The cup row's bar measured about 5 pixels. On a four-across card the bar track comes out near 113px, and the cup row's coloured run is 4.2% of it for Away Days. It was implying a comparison it could not draw, on the row where the comparison matters most. It is gone from the cards, and in the panel there is one bar for the tie only, on a plain 0 to 100% scale with no crop to declare.
  • Both rows on one cropped scale was the reason the bars existed, and the crop was never on screen. It let the two rows stack, which was the stated point; what it also did was put every cup figure in the left sixth of the track. Numbers in a right-aligned column compare perfectly well and cost no height, so the four cards still read against each other at a glance.

What survives from option 03 unchanged: two horizons rather than one, because the catalogue lives in both and their disagreement is the feature; position-aware figures, which is the whole finding of item 05; whole points, never decimals; no real change rather than a fake zero when the movement is inside the noise; and a label rather than a number for the four cards that pay outside the simulation.

What is being merged, and why it is one job

Item 65 says the three boosts do not look pressable: on a phone they stack into full-width panels, which is the shape content takes in this app, and every signal that they are controls fires on hover. Item 05 says a pick is currently a guess, and the odds machinery to fix that already exists. Neither is a reason to touch the other, until you notice what each one costs.

Item 65's best remaining option asks the player to tap twice: once to choose, once to confirm. On its own that is friction bought with nothing but safety. And item 05 has the mirror problem: its readout puts numbers on every card, which is height on the screen that can least afford it, and roughly a second of simulation for an offer the player may take in two seconds. Put them together and each pays the other's bill.

The brief, in the owner's words

"Displaying that additional information would justify a 2 click process to select the boost." That is the whole design: the first tap is not ceremony, it is the request for the numbers. Which also settles what happens on the cards themselves, because the numbers no longer all have to be on them.

What is being built on. Button treatments B (a quiet button in every card) and D (choose, then confirm) from the button comp; options A and C are discarded. Readout option 03 from the odds comp, with the presentation corrected above.

The offer drawn throughout is the one the odds were measured on: the first boost of a run, through the group, Brazil 2002 drawn in the Round of 16, at four cards because Extra Choice tier 2 makes it four and that is the tallest case. Three is the default.

What it buys

The first tap becomes the question and the panel is the answer, which is the brief. Tap through the four in either frame: the cards stay the size they are today, the phone offer is about 340px instead of 570, and the panel is the one place with room for the bar worth drawing. It also gives the loading state a natural home, since the panel opens and the numbers land in it.

What it costs

Comparing four cards means four taps, and that is the honest cost: option 03's own case was that four cards compare at a glance. The counter is that the taps are free and reversible, nothing commits until the button, and two horizons have to be read rather than glanced at in any case.

Option 3 is also what changes item 05's bill, which is the real argument for it

The readout was costed at roughly 950ms for a baseline plus four cards, and a web worker to be honest about it - 190ms a card is fine off the main thread and not fine on it. Computing only the card that was tapped turns that into one 190ms burst per tap, into a panel that has just opened and can hold a spinner without looking stuck. That is the difference between needing a worker and probably not needing one. It is a judgement rather than a free win: 190ms is still a visible stall, so it wants measuring on a real phone before the worker is called off.

Two smaller bills go the same way. The Physio Table re-roll becomes free again (a fresh offer computes nothing until something is tapped, where option 03 re-ran three simulations), and the baseline is the only figure that has to be ready before the screen is.

The cards a number cannot price still get a label

Four of the 32 boosts pay outside the simulation, and printing them at 6% to 6% would say they are worthless when they are not. Option 03's answer carries into all three merges unchanged, and in option 3 it is what the panel says instead of figures:

The three label shapes, on the card and in the panel

A boon declares which of these it is (priced: 'sim' | 'payout' | 'album' | 'hidden', one line beside rarity), and that is the thing that stops a payout card reading as worthless. In option 3 the label is the whole panel for such a card, which is the one place it is cheaper than the other two merges are: no simulation runs at all.

The odds switch: what it is, exactly

It is a saved player preference, and it comes from option 03 rather than from the merge. Both switches on this page are live, so it can be tried rather than read about.

What it is

A setting, remembered between runs, exactly as the bracket's "show the full draw" chevron is. Its control belongs on the offer, in the strip above the boosts, rather than buried in the settings sheet, for the same reason the bracket's chevron is its own control: this is where you would want it.

It is not a difficulty option and it changes nothing about the game. It only decides whether the screen tells you what it knows.

What it hides

Every figure, the baseline included. If you do not want the pick solved, you do not want "31% to beat Brazil" either, so the strip keeps only the switch. In the code this is the whole readout being absent rather than blank, which is also what a slow first frame looks like, so it costs no extra branch.

What stays: the boost names, their descriptions, their rarity, and the label on the four cards that pay outside the simulation, since none of that is an odds figure.

Why it exists at all

Two reasons, and the second is the honest one. Some players will want the pick to stay a guess. And a solved offer may simply be less interesting: four cards with numbers on them is an arithmetic problem rather than a gamble, and the from-to form makes that more true than a bare delta did, because it needs no working out. The switch is the hedge against having built something that makes the game duller.

What it does in each of the three merges

  • In 1 and 2 the cards lose their two rows and go back to being what ships today, plus the button. One tap still takes a boost in 1, two in 2. Nothing else changes.
  • In 3 the panel has nothing to show, which is the whole problem. Turn either switch off above and read what the panel says: the first tap only highlights the card and the second confirms it. That is not broken, but it is the second tap charging for something it is no longer delivering.
Two taps with the odds turned off is friction with nothing behind it

Option 03 keeps an Odds on preference, on the reasoning that some players want the guess and a solved offer is less interesting than an unsolved one. That is right on its own and it collides with this merge: if the numbers are what the second tap buys, turning them off leaves the tap and takes the payment. Three answers, and the third is the recommendation:

Drop the switch - one flow, and the preference the owner might want is gone. Fall back to one tap when the odds are off - honest, and it means the game has two different interaction models for the same screen depending on a setting, which is the kind of thing this codebase deletes rather than adds. Keep both, unchanged, on the ground that the second tap was already justified without the odds: a boost cannot be given back, and today a mis-tap spends the whole round's choice. The odds make the tap pay; they were never the only reason for it.

Both switches on this page are live. Turn one off and the figures go, which is exactly what the component does when the prop is absent.

What the merged item would take

  • A position-aware odds pass. domain/odds.ts gains a sibling to simulateTitleOdds that takes the run's bracket and a per-round XI rather than building its own group. The per-round XI comes from the effect ledger, xiOf(roster, effects, round), which already knows appliesFrom and expiresAfter - that is the whole reason these numbers are right and the naive ones are not. applyBoon is the measurement path and skips startsIn by design.
  • Two figures per boost, and the baseline once. The screens print your chance now and your chance with the boost, so the component needs the base pair and a pair per boon; the delta is never printed, which is what keeps the rounding honest. BoostOffer takes an optional odds map keyed by boon id, so it stays presentational and renders identically with the prop absent, which is what the switch and a slow first frame both look like.
  • A priced field on the catalogue entry, beside rarity, choosing between figures and a label.
  • The button half of item 65, which is small: the chosen card fills pitch-dark (the app's one chosen-cell green) and one btn('primary') below names what it will take. The bar in merge 1 is a <span> wearing btn() and marked aria-hidden rather than a nested button, for the reason PlayerBadge already carries.
  • The XI panel's own Title figure should read from the same place. CupRunScreen computes simulateTitleOdds(activeXi, 600, ...) with no reference to the run's position, so it still includes a group stage you won two rounds ago. Two figures on one screen disagreeing about the same run is worse than one wrong figure.
  • The rarity word's contrast, carried over from item 65. At 9px bold in the tier accents it reads 2.57 / 2.49 / 4.00 on the light panel and the common green misses in the dark theme too, against the 4.5 a label that size needs. The fix has a precedent twice over: amber-ink and pitch-ink exist because a colour as text is a separate value from the same colour as a surface.

Still undecided, and none of it is settled by this page

  • Does the readout spoil The Coin Toss? Its result is derived from the XI and the opponent so that a reload cannot re-roll it, which means a figure would tell you heads or tails before you pick, and the card is nothing else. Drawn here as "not shown until taken". The alternative is to accept the spoiler and retire the card.
  • Does a solved offer stay interesting? Four cards with numbers on them is an arithmetic problem rather than a gamble, and the from-to form makes that more true than a bare delta did, since it needs no working out. The two horizons disagreeing is the only thing working against it.
  • Is Sold Out Stadium still shippable once the screen prices it? Its known hole (free if taken before the final) becomes visible the day this ships. Fix the card, or let "no real change" be the warning.
  • Whether the second tap survives the odds being off - the switch decision above.
