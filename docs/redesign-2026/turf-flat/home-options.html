Front page · five whole-page reworks

The home page says nothing about the career

It sells two things: how a run works, and that stickers exist. A player arriving today is never told that a finished run pays levels and Prestige, that Prestige buys six perk tracks and unlocks 32 boosts, that winning opens a harder tier, or that 130 permanent honours sit across the whole of it. Three of the game's six tabs are invisible from the page that is supposed to sell it.

Five ways to fix it below, drawn whole rather than described. The tactics board and the five sticker cards do not move in any of them: same place, same size, same treatment. What changes around them is the copy, the headline, the order, and what is added in between. Each is a real page, so scroll it as a page.

01 Hold the shape Cheapest

Two blocks, exactly as today. The career arrives as one sentence in the hero and one line of figures under the button.

  • Nothing is added below the fold. The page keeps the length it has, which is what its own history argues for.
  • The strip is the whole of the new furniture: five figures on one mono line, the only place the game's size is stated at all.
  • It tells rather than shows. "Prestige buys perks" is a claim on a page that otherwise demonstrates everything it says.

02 The loop Clearest

A band between the two blocks that draws the actual shape of the game: build, play, earn, spend, and round again one tier harder.

  • It answers "why would I play a second run". That is the one question the page has never addressed, and it is the whole of the career.
  • Four short cards, no icons and no tiles, so it is not the deleted three-beat block coming back in a hat: those restated the hero, these say what it does not.
  • It is a section, and the page's own note argues against one. The reason here is different, but it is still a third block to scroll.

03 Three shelves Recommended

The album stops being the only thing the page keeps. Album, ledger and cabinet stand side by side, each showing a real piece of itself, and the five cards become the album made visible underneath.

  • It shows rather than tells. Three real honours with their real awards, and a shelf of real trophies, do the job the sentence in option 1 only claims.
  • The cards gain a reason to be there. They stop being a separate section and become the end of the first column, which is what they always were.
  • It is the app's own ledger idiom: hairline rules, one accent, earned in green and everything else quiet. Nothing new had to be designed.

04 The ladder Boldest hook

Leads with the roguelike promise: the game gets harder on purpose, and the six tiers are laid out with the real handicap and the real payout.

  • It sells the hook nothing else on the page sells. "Win, then win a harder one" is the reason this is not a one-sitting game.
  • Every number is the real one, straight off the ladder, so the page is making a promise the game keeps rather than a mood.
  • It is the most intimidating page of the five. A first-time visitor meets a locked ladder before they have played a match.

05 Career first Biggest change

The headline itself changes. The page stops promising one cup and promises the cup after that one, with three figures for what a run pays and the shelf as the closing argument.

  • The headline is the only line most people read, and today it promises a single tournament. This is the only option that fixes that.
  • Three big mono figures, no icons, in the type the app already uses for every number. The cards then close the page instead of ending it abruptly.
  • It re-opens a settled decision. "Draft a random XI. Win the cup." has been the line since the start, and the wordmark's own voice is built on it.
