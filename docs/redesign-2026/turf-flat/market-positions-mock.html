The question

You are right that the space is gone.

The market row is 259px wide and four of its five columns are fixed: the flag is 18, the rating 24, the price 52, and the gaps 12. The name gets whatever is left, which is 137px, or 116px once a collectible star sits beside it. One name in the sixty on screen is already clipped, and it is only clipped by three pixels. So the honest starting point is that a position column does not fit and something has to give.

The desktop three-column layout is the only place this is tight. On a phone the market is the full width of the screen, the row is 305px and the name gets 183px, so nothing clips even with 38px added. Whatever is chosen, it is free on a phone and paid for on a desktop.
Why it is worth paying anything

For a winger, the whole top of the list is out of position.

The market filters by every position a player can fill, which is correct: a striker who can play left wing should be buyable there. It also means all sixty rows are eligible and an unknown subset are actually wingers. Of the 654 players the market offers for a left-wing slot, 287 are naturally left wingers. Of the ten dearest, four are. Maradona is an attacking midfielder, Cruyff a striker, Iniesta a central midfielder, and buying any of them costs the chemistry point that item 38 just made real. Nothing on the row says so.

It is not uniform, which is the argument for showing it rather than explaining it once: for a centre-back slot 1,557 of 1,975 are natural, so the mark would be near-silent there and loud exactly where the expensive mistakes are.

The recommendation

C, and it is cheaper than it looks.

Spend the width only on the players who are out of position

The market already filters by every position a player can fill, so every row on screen can legally play the slot. Printing all three of Ronaldinho's positions tells you two things you cannot use and one you can. The one you can use is whether this man plays here or somewhere else, and that is a single word.

  1. A winger in a winger's slot shows nothing and keeps his full 137px. His name is underlined, which is the same signal the roll draft already uses.
  2. Anyone out of position spends 24px to say what he actually is, in the amber that the app already uses for "look at this". Those are the rows where the information changes a decision, so those are the rows that can afford an ellipsis.
  3. It gets quieter as it gets less useful. For a centre-back slot four rows in five are natural, so four in five stay untouched. For a left wing it is the opposite, which is exactly where the mistakes are expensive.

The wording and the underline should be lifted verbatim from the drawn-squad panel rather than reinvented, since that panel has been saying "only placing the player there earns positional chemistry" all along. Option D is worth having as well: it is a filter rather than a display, so it does not compete, and the market's own rule already covers it, that a control appears when it has more than one option or a selection live on it.
