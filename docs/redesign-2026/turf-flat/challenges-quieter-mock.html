Catalogue rework, three options

Quieter Challenges

The catalogue is 130 entries, and today every one of them is painted: a family hue on the left edge, a filled tier chip, a status line in its own colour, and the tifo hard shadow. Nothing recedes, so nothing reads. All three options below spend colour on one thing only, and all three grey out what you have not earned. Same real catalogue, same mock save (28 completed), so they compare like for like.

Where the noise comes from

    01 Ledger Drop the cards. 130 honours want a list, not a grid of tiles.
    • No card, no border, no shadow. Hairline rows inside a family, so the page is type on paper instead of 130 outlined boxes.
    • Family colour appears 12 times, as the rule under each family heading, never on an entry.
    • Tier stops being a colour. Three monochrome pips read as difficulty, which is what the tier actually is while awards are off.
    • Earned is the only ink. Completed entries are full strength with a green tick, everything else is grey, and blocked ones just fade further. No red.

    02 Earned lights up Keep the grid. Colour becomes the reward, not the wallpaper.
    • Unearned cards are grey and flat: tinted surface, hairline border, no shadow, no family edge, tier as an outline chip. They are a backdrop.
    • Completing one turns it on: paper white, the tifo shadow, the family edge, the filled tier chip, the green tick. The page gains colour as you play.
    • Same layout as today, so this is the smallest change: it is ChallengeCard restyled, nothing moved.
    • Blocked entries lose their red. Dashed, faded, a small lock, because "not tracked yet" is not an error.

    03 Shelf Show less. Twelve family rows at rest, opened one at a time.
    • The page starts as 12 strips, each a name, a progress bar and a count. The bar is the only coloured thing, and it is the one place a family hue earns its keep.
    • Open a family and you get its 8 to 16 entries, not 130 at once. The click is real here, try it.
    • Earned first, as a shelf of chips. What you have won reads in one pass, and what is left sits under it as a plain grey checklist.
    • Blocked entries go last under their own quiet subhead, out of the way of the things you can actually get.

    True of all three

    Dropped

    • The family bar on every card. Family is a grouping, and the grouping is already the heading.
    • The filled tier chip on unearned entries, which put 130 saturated badges on a page where none of them meant anything yet.
    • The red "not tracked yet". It reads as broken. Muted plus a lock says the same thing calmly, and the counter's red swatch goes with it.
    • The "Not yet" caption under every open entry. Grey already says it, so 75 copies of the words are pure noise.

    Kept

    • The album-shaped completion header. It is the summary, and the one place a progress gradient belongs.
    • The tier, as difficulty. It survives as pips or as an outline, and only goes full colour when the thing is won (and, with awards on, when it pays).
    • The four filters. Unchanged, and they matter more now: "Available" is the view most players want.
    • The green tick. One accent, one meaning, used 28 times instead of competing with fifteen other hues.

    If you want one: option 2 is the cheapest to ship, since it restyles ChallengeCard and touches nothing else, and greying the unearned does most of the work on its own. Option 3 is the one that scales, because the real problem is 130 things on one screen rather than the colour on any single card, and it is also where a future "daily three" strip would sit naturally. Option 1 is the best pure reading experience and the weakest trophy cabinet: it reads as a spreadsheet of honours, which may be exactly wrong for a screen whose job is to make you want them.
