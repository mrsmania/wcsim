Mondialino typography comparison

What this is

Two renderings of the same four screens. The markup is generated once and dropped into both boards, so the colours, the spacing, the borders, the radii and the tifo shadow are identical down to the pixel. Only the type changes. Anything that looks different is a consequence of the fonts and the way the small labels are set, and nothing else.

What moves on the proposed side

  • The monospace face is retired. Today every rating, price, score, percentage and small caption is set in Spline Sans Mono, which is the single loudest signal that a machine laid the page out. On the proposed side the figures are the text face with proper tabular lining numerals, so columns still line up and nothing reads as a terminal. Recursive is the exception and keeps a monospace, because it has one built into the same family and it is a warm one rather than a technical one.
  • Labels are sentence case. Today they are capitals with up to 0.2em of extra letter spacing. On the proposed side they are written the way a person writes. This is a separate switch in the bar above, so you can hold the fonts still and see how much of the effect is just the capitals.
  • Headings lose a step of weight. Archivo Black at 900 becomes 700, and the tight -0.02em tracking eases to -0.01em.
  • Labels come up six per cent, because lowercase reads smaller than capitals at the same size and would otherwise go quiet.
  • The wordmark stays in capitals on both sides. A logotype is a fixed asset, not a label, so it changes typeface here but never case. That keeps the casing switch a statement about the interface and nothing else.

What does not move

The green, the paper, the hard offset shadow, the card shapes, the pitch, every layout decision, and every word of copy. If the proposed side reads better it is not because anything was tidied up while you were not looking.

The five pairings

  • Rubik and Inter. The recommendation. Rubik has slightly rounded corners and sturdy heavy weights, which suits a football game and is not the face every dashboard uses; Inter carries the tables, line-ups and figures, which is the job it is best in the world at. Two clearly different jobs, two clearly different faces.
  • Inter only. The most restrained answer. One family, hierarchy carried by weight and size alone. Safe, calm, and slightly anonymous.
  • Poppins and Open Sans. The friendliest. Poppins is geometric and round, which reads younger and more casual. It is also currently the most heavily used display face on the web, so it carries a whiff of template.
  • Recursive. One superfamily doing all three jobs, with its own duplexed monospace for the figures. The most distinctive of the five and the most opinionated.
  • Lato and Roboto. The most conventional. Lato's heavy weights are warm and a little humanist; Roboto is the plainest, most invisible text face available.

The screens are fixtures, not live data: a drafted 4-3-3, a group won on seven points, a part-filled honours ledger and a cabinet with four cups in it.
