World Cup Simulator

Challenges a plan

Drafted: 2026-08-18
Status: plan, not built
Roadmap: item 01

The gap. Career Mode has no goals. You play, you earn Prestige, you buy perks, and that is the loop. Challenges give a reason to attempt a particular kind of run rather than the same strongest one, they give Prestige a second faucet, and they are what would fill the trophy cabinet (item 06, which is waiting on this).

The good news is that most of it is already computable. A finished RunState carries the final XI, the outcome, the Ascension played, the boosts taken, and a per-round history with scorelines, how each tie was decided, and the goal events including scorer. Roughly 100 of the 130 challenges below need no new tracking at all.

1. The decision: permanent now, rotating later

Decided 2026-08-18: every challenge is permanent. One shape, one catalogue:

  • Honours (permanent). The whole catalogue, each completable once, each worth a fixed Prestige award and a cabinet badge. This is the long tail that outlasts "reach the next Ascension", and it is the part that makes the cabinet worth opening.

An earlier draft of this plan paired those with Mandates: three rotating picks from the same catalogue at a reward multiplier, refreshed on a period key. That layer is cut from this plan and moved to a roadmap item of its own (daily challenges). The argument for it still stands - a rotation is what makes you build a different XI this week, and it re-offers challenges you would otherwise skip - but it is a second feature stacked on this one, and the catalogue has to exist and be worth completing before anything can rotate through it.

Permanent-first costs the rotating layer nothing. It reads the same catalogue and the same predicates, and adds a period key, the chosen ids and a multiplier, all on CareerState. There is nothing here for it to undo.

2. Model

Pure predicates over a finished run, in domain/challenges.ts. No React, no storage reads, so the whole catalogue is testable in the checks harness.

export type ChallengeFamily =
  | 'silverware' | 'ascension' | 'identity' | 'rating' | 'defence'
  | 'attack' | 'drama' | 'boosts' | 'album' | 'market' | 'shape' | 'career';

export type ChallengeTier = 'bronze' | 'silver' | 'gold';

/** Everything a predicate may look at. Passed in, never read from storage here. */
export interface ChallengeCtx {
  run: RunState;              // the finished run (xi, outcome, ascension, history, ...)
  base: (p: Player) => Player; // the DATASET player, before boon rating deltas
  career: CareerState;        // for streak / lifetime conditions
  album: AlbumState;          // for collection conditions
}

export interface Challenge {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  description: string;        // player-facing, imperative: "Win the cup with ..."
  family: ChallengeFamily;
  tier: ChallengeTier;        // drives the Prestige award
  check: (ctx: ChallengeCtx) => boolean;
}

Evaluation seam. One call, right where the run is already banked: completedIn(ctx) returns the ids newly satisfied (catalogue minus what the career has already claimed). applyRunResult then adds their Prestige to the same wallet perks are bought from, and appends them to career.completedChallenges. That is the whole of the state: the set of ids completed, and the Prestige already paid for them.

Accounts. Challenge state lives on CareerState, so it rides the existing career write and the existing save_career RPC - no new table, no new migration. The award is a client write like the rest of the career (design decision D3, "trust the client with sanity limits"), and it is audited server-side like every other career write.

Three traps, each already a real bug in this codebase 1. Ratings must be judged on the DATASET player. run.xi carries boon deltas baked in (Golden Generation is +2 to the whole XI), so "an XI averaging under 80" would silently drift with the boosts taken. This is exactly the bug the sticker album hit in August; the fix there was BASE_BY_ID, and that is why base() is in the context above rather than left to each predicate.

2. The final XI is not the XI you built. Roster boosts (Transfer, Poach, Wildcard Legend) swap players in mid-run, and run.boostedIds is what separates them. "Win with a single-nation XI" has to say which XI it means - the catalogue below judges the FINAL XI unless the name says otherwise, and identity challenges exclude boosted players so a Wildcard cannot break a themed run by accident.

3. Shootout goals are not goals. KoMatch.pens is separate from userGoals / oppGoals, so clean-sheet predicates must read the scoreline and ignore the shootout - otherwise "The Wall" is unwinnable the moment a tie goes to penalties.

Not recorded yet. Anything marked needs in the catalogue depends on one of these, all small and all additive to RunState at beginRun:

  • How the XI was built - roll or budget, and the budget spent. The market knows both; AUTOFILL currently drops the distinction on the way into the run.
  • Formation and style at kickoff (4-3-3, bal), and the slot each player filled - needed for "in his natural position", which cannot be derived from the XI alone because placedPlayers reorders positions.
  • Chemistry at kickoff (a number, already computed for the panel).
  • Career counters for streaks: consecutive cups, consecutive semi-finals or better, Prestige spent lifetime.
  • Group goal events. The group stores scorelines only, so scorer-based challenges are knockout-only until the group keeps its events too.

5. In the app

Drawn as a comp in docs/redesign-2026/turf-flat/challenges.html, in the same house style as the sticker album: every surface below, plus the full catalogue in its three states (available, completed, not tracked yet).

Where it lives

  • Career hub (CupRunScreen, between runs): a Challenges card beside the perk shop - the completion counter, the Prestige it has paid, and the three most recently completed, each a row with its family colour and its award. A footer link opens the full catalogue.
  • New route /challenges: the whole catalogue, grouped by family, filterable to available / completed / impossible-for-now, with a completion counter in the header exactly like the album's. This is the collection surface, and the closest existing thing to copy is AlbumScreen.
  • Before and during a run: nothing, for now. A build-page strip and in-run chips only work over a handful of targeted challenges, which is exactly what a rotation hands you; 130 permanent ones cannot be a chip row. Both surfaces belong with the rotating layer, and the idea worth keeping for it is the already failed marking: predicates are end-of-run, but many become impossible earlier (conceding kills The Wall the moment it happens), and saying so beats letting a run look winnable when it is not.
  • Run end: completed challenges appear on the ended screen above the score, each with its Prestige, in the same slot the sticker haul uses. This is the reward moment.

Copy rules

  • Descriptions are imperative and concrete: "Win with an XI averaging under 80", never "average rating < 80".
  • House vocabulary: rating not elo, boost not boon. A challenge name is a name, the description carries the rule.
  • No em-dashes anywhere, per the project convention.

6. Build order

Each slice ships behind FEATURES.challenges and is useful alone.

  1. The engine and the free half. domain/challenges.ts with the model, the context builder (including base()), and the ~100 challenges that need no new tracking. Evaluated in applyRunResult; CareerState gains completedChallenges. Run-end screen lists what was completed. Checks harness gets a "every predicate is total and pure" sweep plus hand-built runs for the tricky ones (clean sheets with a shootout, boosted-player exclusion).
  2. The catalogue screen (/challenges) and the hub card, with the completion counter.
  3. The plumbing wave. Build method, budget spent, formation and style, slot positions, chemistry at kickoff, and the career counters - unlocking families J, K and the streak entries in L.
  4. Trophy cabinet (item 06) reads the completed set: badges by family, newest first, with the run that earned each one.

7. Open questions

  • Do challenges apply to Quick Run? Recommendation: no. They are the career layer's spine, and a Quick Run has no Prestige wallet to pay into.
  • Retroactive completion? Recommendation: no for run-shaped challenges (there is no run history detailed enough to re-judge), yes for lifetime ones that read the career counters, which are already accurate.
  • Difficulty and Ascension interaction. A challenge completed at a high Ascension could pay the Ascension multiplier, which would reward doing hard things the hard way. Worth a decision before the reward numbers are tuned.
Plan only. Nothing here is built. The catalogue is a menu to cut from, not a commitment to all 130: slice 1 is the engine plus whatever subset reads well, and the rest is data.
