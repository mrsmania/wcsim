The authenticity pass
making the game stop reading as machine-written

Drafted 2026-09-05
Revised after review, same day
Status awaiting five decisions
The report was "the page currently screams made by AI". This is the audit, the evidence, and 24 numbered changes in four waves, with the replacement copy written out so it can be judged on the page instead of imagined. Version 2. Two outside reviewers, one front-end and one copy, took the first draft apart. Both were right about things, one of them was right about the thing this document was most confident about, and Section 9 records what changed and what I pushed back on.

Section 0What the goal is, and what it is not

Two different goals hide inside "stop looking AI generated", and they pull in opposite directions.

  1. Stop reading as generic. The page looks like the output of a model that was given no brief. That costs trust with exactly the audience this game needs, and every action below addresses it.
  2. Pretend no AI was involved. Off the table. The project is roughly 99% agent-written by its own account, and the indie audience treats undisclosed AI as a breach when it surfaces later. It surfaces later.

The thesis, corrected

The first draft of this document said specificity is the anti-slop signal and built everything on it. The copy reviewer took that apart and was right to. Facts alone produce accurate trivia that nobody finishes reading, and a "three checkable facts above the fold" target would have been a quota, filled the way quotas are.

Look at what makes the good copy already in this product good. "About 88. Three genuine greats, or spread it and take nine good ones." The number does nothing there. The second clause does everything, because it tells the reader what the choice will cost them. "Enough to refuse a squad with nobody you need." No figure at all.

The working rule for the whole pass: a sentence is unmovable when it commits to something about the player's experience that a competitor would not dare put in writing. "Most of your XI will be players you settled for" cannot be lifted onto another product and has no statistics in it. Specificity is the floor. Nerve is the thing.

Section 1Who this page is for, and the sentence that is backwards

The audit missed this and the copy reviewer found it. It outranks every AI tell in the document, because it is a problem the page would have even if a person had written it by hand.

The headline promises the opposite of the game. Draft your dream XI says you will assemble the team of your dreams. You will not. A squad comes up at random, you take one player from it, and it never comes up again. What you finish with is a compromise, and the compromise is the entire game. The copy is selling a fantasy the product deliberately withholds, which is worse than a generated sentence: it sets up the wrong expectation in the first four seconds.

Three audiences are reachable and they do not want the same page.

Section 2What readers flag, as of 2026

Checked against current sources instead of from training data, because the tells move. Em dashes were the canonical giveaway two years ago and the people who study this now consider them unreliable. A note on source quality: Nielsen Norman Group and Wikipedia's own detection guide are worth citing. Most of the rest of what a search returns is content marketing about content marketing, and it is listed in Section 11 for completeness rather than as authority.

Design

  • Distributional convergence. Independent projects land on the same page, because a model emits the most probable pattern: Inter, a blue-to-purple gradient, an oversized hero with a vague headline, cards at a uniform 16px radius.
  • Uniformity. Identical padding, identical radius, identical card heights throughout. A design system creates hierarchy through deliberate variation; machine output makes everything the same size because sameness is the safe prediction.
  • Ornamental motion. The same fade or lift on every element, applied because it was available.
  • Default icon sets. Lucide ships with the most common AI scaffolding and has become part of the fingerprint.
  • The countersignal is visible authorship. NN/g's finding: users now read imperfection, texture and a credited maker as evidence of care.

Copy

  • Tidy triplets. One published threshold is more than one polished triplet per 200 words.
  • The antithesis habit. "X rather than Y", "not X, it's Y". The loudest prose tell of 2026 and the one this document's own first draft was full of.
  • Low burstiness. Sentences cluster around one length.
  • Puffery. Ordinary facts described as significant.
  • The vocabulary. Unlock, seamless, elevate, dive into, delve, testament, game-changer.
  • Everything explained. A heading, then a sentence restating the heading, in every slot, because a model fills every slot it is given.

What is sound, and should be defended. Away from the front page the design system holds up under exactly the tests the research proposes. No Inter: the stack is Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk and Spline Sans Mono. No blue-to-purple gradient: one green identity with measured contrast figures. No uniform card grid: a hard offset shadow taken from a brutalist tifo study. The radius histogram is 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 pixels, chosen per component. Those are decisions a generator does not make, and the brief here is corrective.

Section 4The voice already exists, with one qualification

The versus screens are written in a dry, concrete voice that no model produces unprompted. The best sentence in the product explains why a room needs an account:

"It needs an account, because the others have to know who they beat and the result has to live somewhere none of you can edit."

Also: "Code only. Nobody can find it, or even confirm it exists." And "Enough to refuse a squad with nobody you need."

The qualification, which the first draft got wrong. That voice addresses somebody who has already decided to play and is now configuring. It is a choosing voice: second person, consequence first, no persuasion needed. A hero addresses somebody who has decided nothing. Copying the room voice onto the cover produces something correct, flat and unpersuasive. So it is the floor for wave 1 and the specification for waves 2 and 3.

The first draft also cited the settings sheet as part of that standard. It is not. The settings sheet contains "Night-match theme for low-light play", which is atmosphere doing the job of information, and the sweep in A18 should catch it.

Section 5The actions

Wave 1 · the front page · one and a half days · carries most of the effect

A1 Rewrite the hero around the constraint

Four options. None is a coloured-payoff couplet. All are true. The first three came out of the copy review and beat everything in the first draft, which offered three variations on the shape it had just condemned.

Option 1 · the mechanic, dramatised · recommended
Brazil 1970 comes up. You may take one player from it.
Then it is gone, another squad comes up, and you do that ten more times. Whatever eleven you end up with plays a World Cup, minute by minute, and it will not be the team you wanted.
Puts the reader inside the decision in six words and assumes they know what Brazil 1970 means, which flatters the football audience without locking out anybody else. The setup is longer than the payoff, inverting the diagnosed rhythm. The last clause is the honest promise the current headline gets backwards.
Option 2 · the anti-hype one, if you are brave
Most of your XI will be players you settled for.
That is the game. A World Cup squad comes up at random, you take one man, and it never comes up again. Eleven picks out of 9,625 footballers, then a tournament that does not care how it looked on paper.
No figure in the headline and still impossible to lift onto another product. The riskiest option and the one most likely to be remembered.
Option 3 · the collection angle
Draft Maradona and you keep the card.
115 of them exist, one for every player in the game good enough to be worth one. The catch is getting him: a squad comes up at random, you take one player, and Argentina 1986 is not coming round again.
Leads with the drawn art. Pick this one only if the album is the hook you want to lead with, and only after T9 is settled.
Option 4 · the low-risk one
Eleven picks, fifteen World Cups, one squad at a time.
A national team comes up at random and you take a player from it. Then it is gone. Whatever you assemble plays a group stage and a knockout, revealed goal by goal.
One line, no break, no payoff, no colour trick. Boring in the correct way.

Rule for the subhead, whichever headline wins: one sentence over 20 words and one under 10, in either order. Never three clauses. Never a comma-separated list of nouns.

Dropped from the first draft: its three directions, including the Morocco one. "Morocco brought 19 men to Mexico in 1970. So this game has 19 of them." is a true fact with no stake in it for the reader. It belongs on the colophon, where curiosity is the point.

A2 Delete the three icon beats, and put the game itself there

Deleting alone leaves a visitor knowing about a draft and a tournament, and knowing nothing about the career, the 130 honours, the trophy cabinet, the transfer market or playing another person. Five of the six tabs go unmentioned. The four-second question survives that; the eight-second "is this worth an evening" question does not.

So the replacement is a recommendation and not a fallback: one wide band showing the game in motion, with two or three short annotations pointing at real things in it.

Best version: a four-second loop of the live match reveal, goals arriving minute by minute on a scoreline nobody can change. That is the asset the copy keeps trying to describe in words, and it is a screen recording of software that already exists. Second best: a still of the board mid-draft, annotated.

A3 Put the front page back on the design system

The hero moves to the house radius and the house shadow token. The 10px tiles die with A2. This is close to a one-line change, it removes the only two orphan radii in the codebase, and it should land before any copy does.

A4 Show the tactics board on a phone

The board is the page's strongest visual claim to authorship and it is desktop-only. Give it a mobile rendering: the board above the words at a wider crop, or a cropped half-board showing five badges. The scrim needs re-shaping for that layout, since it currently fades toward an element that is absent below 1120px.

Then audit the page at 390, 768, 1024 and 1440, in both themes, before anything ships. The first draft of this plan contained no responsive check and no dark-theme check, on a page whose scrim was a live dark-theme contrast bug three days ago.

A5 Settle the sticker crop, then promote the art

Blocker first. Ratify or revert the 65% crop marked "do not ship". Enlarging the cards on the cover makes that crop the most visible thing on the site.

Then: fewer cards, larger, in colour from the start, with a line that says what they cost to get. Both figures derived at render time and never typed in.

Now
Chase the legends
Draft an all-time great and the sticker is yours to keep. These five are the rarest of all.
Then
115 players are worth a card
Three of them are rated 99. Draft one and it is yours for good.

A6 Say what the button does, truthfully

Build your XI now plus an arrow becomes Build an XI. Drop the "now" and drop the arrow.

The first draft proposed "Roll a squad" and was wrong: pressing that button lands on the setup panel, where the first controls are formation and style and rolling is the third one down. Either use the honest label above, or make the claim true by landing on a default formation with the roll button focused, which is a small change and the better of the two.

A7 Drop the ornamental hover

The lift and the greyscale reveal go with A5. If the cards want motion, give it a meaning: the card sits up slightly when it is one already in your album.

A8 Rewrite the closing strip as a dated changelog line

The current line is a triplet and a boast. Replace it with evidence that somebody was working on this recently, which is difficult to fake and cheap to keep.

Then
Last change, 3 September: the 2026 field went in, all 48 squads.
The date is the credibility. The first draft appended a joke about 66 wrong shirt numbers; the wit draws attention to itself and costs more than it returns. Save the corrections for the colophon, where they are the substance.

Wave 2 · the front door · half a day · highest ratio in the document

A9 Write the share card

Four meta tags and one image. This is how the product actually travels, since a duel invitation is a link by design.

The image should be a strip of four sticker cards in colour at 1200 by 630, and not a screenshot of the interface. A drawn card is unmistakable at thumbnail size; a UI screenshot is not.

Draft, to be finalised with whichever hero option wins:

  • Title: Mondialino - draft an XI from 15 World Cups
  • Description: A random World Cup squad comes up. You take one player, and it never comes up again. Do that eleven times, then play the tournament.

Wave 3 · the voice sweep · one day · the rest of the app

A10 Delete the eyebrow where it is a synonym, keep it where it is a parent

Four of six restate their own title. The squad browser's is a real breadcrumb and stays.

The first draft proposed breaking the header template on two screens "because applying it exhaustively is what machines do". The front-end reviewer was right to reject that: it contradicts a decision recorded on 2026-08-23, and deliberately inconsistent chrome reads as something somebody forgot. The defect is that the eyebrow is empty, and this rule fixes it for a reason the next screen can follow.

A11 Cut the subtitles that paraphrase, keep the ones that state a rule

Delete a subtitle if it paraphrases its heading. Keep it if it states a rule, a cost or a consequence that appears nowhere else on the screen.

The first draft said "expect to delete more than is comfortable", which is calibrated on a returning player and would have taken out the line telling a newcomer that duplicates are a currency and Prestige is spendable. An agent at 2am follows that instruction enthusiastically.

A12 Rewrite the sign-in email

The reader has already decided to trust the site, so the room voice is right here. Draft:

Here is your code. It works for the next hour, on this device or another one.

123456

If you did not ask for it, nobody got in and there is nothing to do.

A13 Rewrite the empty states as facts, in the room voice

The versus and cabinet empty states are already right. The rest should match: say what is not there and why, in one sentence, without instructing the player to go and fix it.

A14 Sweep the vocabulary, including outside the source tree

Unlock, seamless, elevate, dive into, delve, testament, game-changer, plus the local offenders: run the gauntlet, just like the real thing, chase, dream XI, night-match theme.

The sweep has to cover the email templates, the site's head and the readme. None of those sits under the source directory, which is where a sweep looks by default.

A15 Redraw or retire the navigation icons

Do not rip the icon library out of 24 files. Two proportionate options: redraw the six navigation marks as inline SVG in the app's own football vocabulary, which already includes corner arcs, penalty spots and a centre circle; or drop icons from the desktop row entirely, since it is labels with an underline already, and keep them only in the phone bar where they carry state.

Wave 4 · authorship and enforcement · one day

A16 Write the colophon, and put the disclosure in the footer

One page reachable from the footer, in the first person: the three sources and what each is for, how the ratings were argued out, and the corrections. The corrections are the part nobody can fake. 66 shirt numbers wrong and fixed. A keeper in the 1998 South Africa squad who never travelled. Three squads that are short because the country brought fewer men. The gaps, stated plainly: positions have not been swept the way names and numbers have.

A colophon linked from the footer is a paper trail, not a disclosure. If the argument is that volunteering reads better than being found out, it has to be visible without volunteering to look. So one always-visible line in the footer, and the paragraph on the page behind it.

Do not write a word of this until the provenance is confirmed by the owner. The copy reviewer proposed a footer line claiming the cards are drawn by hand and the ratings were set by people. The second half is doubtful: the project's own notes describe the raters as agents working in pairs. The first half is plausible and unproven. What the files themselves show: every card source is a Photoshop document, Windows, edited in sessions of three minutes to two hours between June and September 2026, with no generator tag on any of them. That is consistent with a person working, and it is not proof. A claim of human authorship that turns out to be false is worse than making no claim at all, which is the same reasoning that rejects an AI-free badge in A24. Decision D4.

A17 Credit somebody, and date the thing

The footer carries a disclaimer and a country. Add a name or a handle, a year, and the link to the colophon. Keep the FIFA line.

A18 Add a slop sweep to the checks suite

The build already fails on em dashes across 194 files. The same mechanism should fail on the vocabulary from A14 and on the antithesis template in any user-facing string.

With a vacuity guard, per the standing convention: the check fails if it finds nothing to scan. Budget half a day and not two hours: separating copy from comments and code is the trap the em-dash check already fell into, when it failed on its own explanation.

A19 Write the voice rule into the project instructions

Agents write nearly all the copy here, so the rule is the durable fix and A18 is only the enforcement. Next to the no-em-dashes rule, quoting the versus screens as the reference:

  • Concrete over atmospheric. Say the consequence, not the mood.
  • No triplets.
  • No "X rather than Y" and no "not X, it's Y".
  • No superlative that is not a measurement.
  • No subtitle that paraphrases its heading.
  • No sentence that could sit on another game's page.
  • Read it aloud. If three sentences in a row are the same length, one of them is wrong.

Section 6Guardrails

  • Three button designs, and that is a ceiling. New copy does not get a new button.
  • Contrast is measured. The hero scrim, the amber headline and the dark-surface button rendering exist because numbers said so, and the checks recompute them from the tokens. Touching the hero's colours means touching those figures.
  • No em dashes. Enforced by the build.
  • Every figure derived. No typed-in counts of squads, players, tournaments or cards. This has gone wrong repeatedly, and the top ratings moved again while this document was being written.
  • Copy is content, not layout. A shorter headline is not permission to grow the hero.
  • Claim nothing about provenance that has not been confirmed. See D4.

Section 8How to tell whether it worked

  1. The cover test. Hide the wordmark and the green. If the remaining words could sit on another game's page, they are still generated copy.
  2. The stake test. Does the hero commit to something about the player's experience that a competitor would not put in writing? Facts alone do not pass this.
  3. Truthfulness. Does the headline describe the game that exists? The current one does not.
  4. Triplets. Under one per 200 words on the front page. Currently three in about 120.
  5. Read it aloud. No three consecutive sentences of the same length.
  6. The link test. Paste the URL into a chat and look at what appears.
  7. The build. Checks pass, including the new sweep, with the contrast figures unchanged.
