The authenticity pass
making the game stop reading as generated

Written 2026-09-05
Branch claude/page-authenticity-polish
Status for review
The report was "the page currently screams made by AI". It does, and the cause is almost entirely copy structure rather than visual design: the turf-flat look is distinctive and holds up, while the words on top of it are written in the exact shape a language model produces when nobody tells it what to say. This is the audit, the evidence behind it, and 22 numbered changes in four waves, with the copy written out so it can be judged rather than imagined.

Section 0The one thing to settle first

There are two different goals hiding in the phrase "stop looking AI generated", and they need separating before any of the work below is worth doing.

  1. Stop reading as generic. The page currently looks like every other page produced by a model with no brief. This is a real product problem, it costs trust, and every action in this document addresses it.
  2. Pretend no AI was involved. This one is not available and should not be attempted. The project is roughly 99% agent-written by its own account, and the indie audience treats undisclosed AI as a trust breach when it surfaces later, which it does.
The recommendation is to go the other way and make the process an asset. The thing that is genuinely rare here is not a human hand on the CSS, it is the research: 416 squads sourced three ways, positions read off actual match line-ups, 66 shirt numbers found wrong and corrected, a South African keeper removed from the 1998 squad because he never travelled. No generated page has that behind it, and no competitor can produce it in an afternoon. Specificity is the anti-slop signal. Everything below is a variation on replacing a summary with a fact.

Section 1What readers actually flag, as of 2026

Checked against current sources rather than from memory, because the tells move: em dashes, for instance, were the canonical giveaway two years ago and are now considered unreliable by the people who study this.

Design signals

  • Distributional convergence. Models emit the most probable pattern, so independent projects land on the same page: Inter, a blue-to-purple gradient, an oversized hero with a vague headline, and a row of cards at a uniform 16px radius.
  • Uniformity as the fingerprint. Identical padding, identical radius, identical card heights throughout. Real design systems create hierarchy through deliberate variation; machine output makes everything the same size because sameness is the safe prediction.
  • Default polish motion. The same generic fade or lift on every element, applied because it is available rather than because anything needed emphasis.
  • The countersignal is visible authorship. Nielsen Norman Group's finding is that in an AI-saturated market users now read imperfection, texture, asymmetry and a credited maker as evidence of care. Handmade is becoming a trust signal, not a style.

Copy signals

  • Tidy triplets. Everything in threes. The threshold offered by one detection guide is more than one polished triplet per 200 words.
  • Low burstiness. Sentences cluster around one length. Human writing runs long, then very short.
  • Puffery. Ordinary facts described as significant. Wikipedia's own guide calls this out as the most reliable tell in prose.
  • The reversal template. "It's not just X, it's Y."
  • The blacklist vocabulary. Unlock, seamless, elevate, dive into, delve, game-changer, testament to. Individually harmless; in a cluster they read as machine.
  • Everything explained. A heading, then a sentence restating the heading, on every section, because the model fills every slot it is given.

What is NOT wrong, and should be defended in review: the visual system itself. There is no Inter, no blue-to-purple gradient, no shadcn card. The type stack is Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk and Spline Sans Mono; the palette is a single green identity; the signature is a hard offset shadow borrowed from a brutalist tifo study; the board is a real SVG tactics diagram. Every one of those is a decision a generator does not make. The design brief here is corrective, not a redesign.

Section 3The voice already exists in the product

This is the most useful finding of the audit and it makes the copy work cheap. The room creation form and the settings sheet are already written in a dry, specific, unpuffed voice that no model produces unprompted:

  • "Considered. Time to read the market, or the squad you were dealt."
  • "About 88. Three genuine greats, or spread it and take nine good ones."
  • "Code only. Nobody can find it, or even confirm it exists."
  • "Enough to refuse a squad with nobody you need."
  • "Your cups were won before line-ups were kept, so there is nobody to list yet."

Short. Concrete. Occasionally funny. Never explains itself twice. The marketing surfaces are the only ones written in brochure voice, which means the job is not inventing a voice, it is propagating the one that is already there onto the four screens that lost it. That is also the single strongest argument that this pass will land: the target is a sample of the product's own writing, not a style imported from outside.

Section 4The actions

Wave 1 · The front page · roughly a day · carries most of the effect

A1 Replace the hero headline with one flat sentence and one fact

The coloured-payoff couplet goes. What replaces it is a plain statement of what the game is, followed by a specific true thing that no generator could have produced. Three directions, the owner picks one; all three are written against real dataset figures.

Now
Draft your dream XI.
Win the World Cup.
Spin real squads from every World Cup since 1970, pick your eleven one slot at a time, then run the gauntlet - group stage to final, live and minute by minute.
Direction A · the ledger (recommended)
Eleven players.
Fifteen World Cups to find them in.
A national squad comes up at random, you take one man from it, and it does not come back. Repeat ten times, then play the tournament with what you ended up with.
Direction B · the boast
Morocco brought 19 men to Mexico in 1970.
So this game has 19 of them.
416 squads, 9,625 players, every one rated by hand. Draft eleven of them and try to win the thing.
Direction C · flat
Draft an XI out of 56 years of World Cups.
Then win one.
The squads are real, the ratings are ours, and the tournament is played out minute by minute whether it goes your way or not.

Why A is the recommendation: it says what actually happens in the game, which the current headline does not, and the second line carries a real number without reading as a statistic. B is the most distinctive and the most likely to be loved or hated, so it belongs to the owner rather than to me. C is the safe one and is still a large improvement.

The subhead loses the triplet either way. One long sentence, one short, uneven on purpose.

A2 Delete the three icon beats outright

Not rewrite. Delete. They restate the hero, they are the most recognisable generated section on the page, and the space is worth more to the sticker art. This is the single highest-value change in the document and it removes code rather than adding any.

If something has to sit there, the replacement is not three of anything: one wide band showing the actual board mid-draft, with two or three short annotations pointing at real things on it. Showing the product beats describing it, which is also the standing advice for game landing pages.

A3 Promote the sticker art and let it be in colour

115 drawn cards is the project's only genuinely handmade asset and the research says visible craft is now the trust signal. Show fewer, larger, in colour, and say what they cost to get.

Now
Chase the legends
Draft an all-time great and the sticker is yours to keep. These five are the rarest of all.
Five small cards, grey until hovered.
Then
115 of them are worth a sticker
Three players in the game are rated 99. Draft one and the card is yours, drawn by hand, for good.
Three or four cards at full size, in colour, slightly unevenly placed.

Both figures are derived at render time, never typed in. The dataset has moved nine times and every hardcoded count in this project's history has gone stale quietly.

A4 Say what the button does

Build your XI now plus an arrow glyph becomes Roll a squad, which is literally the next thing that happens when it is pressed. Drop the arrow: it is the most common ornamental icon in generated interfaces and it carries no information the button does not already have. The Continue action keeps its arrow if the owner prefers, since that one genuinely means "resume, forward".

A5 Turn the closing strip into a dated changelog line

The current line is a triplet and a boast. Replace it with the most human artefact available: evidence that somebody was working on this recently.

Now
From 1970 to 2026. 15 World Cups, every squad, one trophy.
Then
Last change: the 2026 field went in, all 48 squads. Before that, 66 shirt numbers were wrong and are not any more.

Sourced from a small hand-maintained list of dated entries rather than generated, and linked to the colophon in A11.

A6 Break the vertical rhythm on purpose

Hero, gap, section, gap, section, gap, closing strip, all at the same spacing, is what makes the page read as poured out of a template. Vary it: let the sticker band run wider than the text column, let one section sit tight under the one above it, and give the hero an uneven internal balance rather than a centred clamp. The research names uniform spacing directly as the fingerprint; this is the cheapest correction to it.

A7 Drop the ornamental hover

The 300ms lift and the grayscale-to-colour reveal go with A3. If the cards want motion, it should be one thing that means something, for instance the card sitting up very slightly when it is the one already in your album, which is information rather than decoration.

Wave 2 · The voice sweep · half a day · the rest of the app

A8 Kill every subtitle that restates its heading

One pass over the six destinations. The test is blunt: cover the subtitle, and if the heading plus the content below still answers the question, the subtitle goes. Expect to delete more than is comfortable. Explaining a screen that explains itself is a generated habit, and this app already has screens that do it well with nothing.

A9 Break the kicker template on at least two screens

Six destinations, six identical kicker-over-title headers, three of them possessive in the same shape. Give the album and the squad browser their own treatment, or none at all. The point is not that the pattern is bad, it is that applying it exhaustively is what machines do.

A10 Rewrite the empty states as facts, in the room voice

The versus and cabinet empty states are already right. The others should match: state what is not there and why, in one sentence, without instructing the player to go and do something about it.

A11 Cut the blacklist vocabulary, once, everywhere

Sweep for the cluster the detection guides name: unlock, seamless, elevate, dive into, delve, testament, game-changer, plus this project's own local offenders (run the gauntlet, just like the real thing, chase, dream XI). Replace each with the concrete thing it was standing in for, or delete the sentence.

Wave 3 · The colophon · half a day · the largest single credibility gain

A12 Write the colophon page: how the ratings were made

One page, reachable from the footer, written in the first person. It is the strongest anti-slop asset the project has and it costs nothing but writing, because the material is already in the repository.

  • The three sources, in order, and what each one is for: the squad lists, each player's own article for the position, and the printed match line-ups for who actually played where.
  • That ratings were authored in pairs and reconciled, and that the pairs agreed to within five points on every player in a tournament.
  • The corrections, which are the part nobody can fake: 66 shirt numbers wrong and fixed, a keeper in the 1998 South Africa squad who never travelled, three squads that are short because the country really did bring fewer men.
  • The known gaps, stated plainly. Positions have not been swept the way names and numbers have. A dataset that admits what it has not checked reads as written by somebody who checked things.

This page is also where AI involvement is disclosed, in one calm paragraph, on the project's own terms rather than as a discovery somebody else makes. See the decision in Section 6.

A13 Put a person in the footer

One line, a name or a handle, a year, and a link to the colophon. Credited authorship is named by the research as a trust signal, and the footer currently carries a flag and nothing else.

A14 Give the dataset a visible changelog

A short dated list on the colophon page: what was added or corrected and when. It feeds A5, it is honest, and a page with a maintenance history is structurally hard to mistake for a generated artefact.

Wave 4 · Keeping it · two hours · so this does not grow back

A15 Add a slop sweep to the checks suite

This repository already fails its build on em dashes across 194 files. The identical mechanism should fail on the blacklist vocabulary and on the reversal template ("not just ... but") in any user-facing string. It is the project's own idiom applied to a house rule that currently exists only as taste.

With a vacuity guard, per the standing convention: the check must fail if it finds nothing to scan, or a refactor that moves copy out of reach turns it into a check that passes by reading zero strings.

A16 Write the voice rule into the project instructions

Four lines, next to the no-em-dashes rule, quoting the versus form as the reference: concrete over atmospheric, no triplets, no subtitle that restates its heading, no sentence that could appear on any other game's page. Agents write almost all the copy in this project, so the rule is the durable fix and the sweep in A15 is only the enforcement.

A17 Read the page out loud before shipping it

Not a joke and not a metaphor: burstiness is the tell that survives every rewrite, and the only reliable detector for it is a human ear. If three sentences in a row come out the same length, one of them is wrong.

Section 5Guardrails

Do not break these while rewriting.
  • Three button designs, and that is a ceiling. New copy does not get a new button. The hero CTAs are the app's own two tones rendered for a dark ground.
  • Contrast is measured, not judged. The hero scrim, the amber headline and the dark-surface button rendering all exist because numbers said so, and the checks suite recomputes them from the tokens. A colour change to the hero is a change to those figures.
  • No em dashes. Existing house rule, enforced by the build.
  • Every figure is derived. No typed-in counts of squads, players, tournaments or cards. This has gone wrong repeatedly.
  • Copy is content, not layout. Shorter headlines must not be compensated for by growing the hero. The one-line header cost the tagline six pixels of width and that arithmetic still holds.

Section 7How to tell whether it worked

  1. The cover test. Hide the wordmark and the green. If the remaining words could sit on any other game's page, they are still generated copy. Every sentence should be impossible to move to another product.
  2. The triplet count. Under one per 200 words on the front page. Currently three in about 120.
  3. Sentence lengths. Read aloud; no three consecutive sentences of the same length.
  4. The specificity count. At least three checkable facts above the fold that are true of this dataset and no other.
  5. The build. The checks suite passes, including the new sweep, and the contrast figures are unchanged.
